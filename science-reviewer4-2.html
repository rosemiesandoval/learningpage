📖 Changes in Materials — Notes

🧪 Physical and Chemical Change
Physical ChangeRetains composition and identity; changes in size, volume, shape, or phase of matter
Chemical ChangeOne substance changes into a completely different form; e.g. rust forming on iron
Evidence of Chemical ChangeColor change, bubble/gas formation (e.g. vinegar + baking soda = CO₂), and release of energy (heat/light)
🍂 Decomposition
DecompositionProcess by which substances are broken down into simpler forms
FactorsTemperature, humidity, exposure to elements, and access by detritivores and decomposers
Organic MaterialsCome from once-living organisms (e.g. fallen leaves, rotten meat); make soil fertile when decomposed
DecomposersBacteria — break down organic material through chemical processes
DetritivoresEarthworms — directly feed on decaying matter
Temperature EffectLow temp slows bacterial growth (refrigeration); high temp speeds decomposition, extreme heat kills decomposers (boiling)
💡 Useful Effects of Changes
EvaporationLiquid water changes to water vapor due to heat
CondensationWater vapor changes into liquid droplets at cooler altitudes, forming clouds
PrecipitationWater released back to the ground as rain, snow, or hail
PenicillinDiscovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928; bacteria-killing substance from Penicillium mold
Ammonia/FertilizerDiscovered by a German chemist in 1909, made from nitrogen; used as fertilizer
NylonDiscovered by Dr. Wallace Carothers in the 1930s as a silk substitute; first used for stockings
Synthetic RubberInvented because natural rubber cracks in cold weather; flexible and waterproof
PetroleumMixture of chemicals: gasoline, kerosene, diesel oil, lubricating substances
⚠️ Harmful Effects and Contamination
ChlorineUsed to kill germs in drinking water; must be used correctly to avoid harm
Water PollutionCaused mainly by sewage and industries; leads to diarrhea, cholera; oil spills harm birds, fish, and plants
Carbon MonoxideTasteless, odorless, colorless gas; stops blood from carrying oxygen; deadly indoors
Indoor Air PollutionFrom combustion by-products, biological contaminants, pesticides, evaporating chemicals, airborne dust
Acid RainFormed when nitrogen/sulfur oxides from burning fuel react with water vapor; damages metal, marble, water life
Soil ContaminationOveruse of fertilizers/insecticides makes soil infertile; chemicals can seep into groundwater
♻️ The Five Rs
ReduceUse less; refuse unneeded items, buy in bulk, walk/bike/carpool
ReuseDonate, repair, or reuse items in projects instead of throwing them away
RecycleBring recyclables to collection companies or junk shops
RethinkChoose eco-friendly, biodegradable, or rechargeable options
RecoverMake compost from biodegradable scraps; can even be a source of energy
🗑️ Waste Segregation & Recycling
Waste SegregationSorting garbage to reduce, reuse, and recycle
BiodegradableFood, garden, animal, and human waste; paper — decomposes naturally
NonbiodegradableCans, bottles, plastics, rubber, glass, metal — do not easily decay
Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (2000)Philippine law that prohibits burning of garbage
Reactive WastesToxic/hazardous, e.g. lithium batteries, sodium metals, aerosol cans
RecyclingConverts used materials into new products; saves energy and reduces pollution
MRFMaterials Recovery Facility — collects and segregates waste materials
🔍 Physical and Chemical Properties
Physical PropertiesObserved/measured directly: color, shape, size, texture, melting/boiling points, hardness, elasticity, malleability, ductility, brittleness
Chemical PropertiesObservable only during a chemical reaction
CombustibilityAbility to catch fire and keep burning strongly
FlammabilityAbility to burn easily in the presence of oxygen
ReactivityAbility to react chemically with another substance, forming something new
ToxicityDegree to which a material can damage a living organism
BiodegradabilityAbility to decompose due to microorganisms
✅ True or False — Key Reminders
FALSEPhysical change alters composition → it actually retains composition and identity.
FALSEVinegar + baking soda is a physical change → it's a chemical change (produces CO₂ gas).
FALSEPlastics/glass decompose faster than paper/food → the opposite is true.
FALSEBacteria can be seen with the unaided eye → they are microscopic.
FALSELow temperature speeds up bacterial growth → it actually slows it down.
FALSEEvaporation turns vapor into droplets → that describes condensation instead.
FALSEMixing chemicals is always safe → some combinations produce toxic fumes, fire, or explosions.
FALSECarbon monoxide has a strong smell and bright color → it is tasteless, odorless, and colorless.
FALSEBurning domestic waste is legal in the Philippines → it violates the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000.
FALSEKitchen wastes should be mixed with other garbage → they should be segregated instead.
FALSENonbiodegradable materials undergo decay easily → they do not easily decay.
FALSEToxicity refers to how easily something decomposes → that's biodegradability; toxicity is about harm to organisms.
