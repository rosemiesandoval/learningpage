Anointing of the Sick

We Believe

During His public life, Jesus reached out to those who were suffering from sickness. He healed them physically and spiritually.

Today, Jesus continues to be a Healer through the Catholic Church. His healing presence is experienced in the sacrament of anointing of the sick. Besides reconciliation, anointing of the sick is also a sacrament of healing. The Church gives it to the sick, aged, and dying. Jesus brings physical and spiritual healing through this sacrament. It encourages the sick to trust in God. It also gives them strength to resist temptation and anxiety about death. (CFC 1867, CCC 1499)

The following are the key elements in the sacrament of anointing of the sick:

  1. Prayer and Scripture reading which serves as a source of comfort in times of pain and suffering caused by sickness

  2. Confession which reconciles the sick with God, the Church, and others

  3. Laying on of hands by the priest which is a sign of the healing touch of Christ and the care of the Church community

  4. Anointing with blessed oil or chrism on the forehead and hands which provides the sick with the grace of the Holy Spirit as well as mental, physical, emotional, and spiritual strength

  5. Holy Communion or the Final Eucharist called Holy Viaticum which is given to the dying Christian. It vividly pictures Christ as leading and accompanying him or her into heaven. (CFC 1873)

All of us have experienced sickness at some point in our lives. Sickness has both positive and negative effects on us. It helps us mature in faith. It helps us see what matters more in life. Faith makes us understand better the mystery of suffering and therefore enables us to bear pain more bravely. On the other hand, sickness can lead to anguish or depression. It makes us feel useless, afraid, insecure, and helpless. It tempts us to lose faith and hope. It makes us blame God and ourselves. As God's children, we are called to show kindness, patience, care, support, gentleness, and love to those who are sick. We should visit them and pray for them.

We Remember

All people need and want special care and attention when they are sick. They need understanding, patience, comfort, care, compassion, support, and love. Sometimes, our simple words, actions, or mere presence could already bring healing to them, maybe not physically but psychologically. These can encourage and strengthen them to face their suffering and pain.

The sacrament of anointing of the sick celebrates God's healing and strength. The Church gives it to the sick, the aged, and dying. Jesus brings physical and spiritual healing through this sacrament.

We Believe

Since the early days of the Church, the Christian communities have continued the healing ministry of Jesus. Special signs were used to bring the healing presence of Jesus to those who were sick and dying.

Today, the Church still uses the same signs to show to sick people that Jesus is present and healing them. In the sacrament of anointing of the sick especially in the two gestures of anointing with oil and the laying on of hands, the Church hopes that the sick would recover and may be given strength by the power of the Holy Spirit (CFC 1830–1831).

The anointing of oil is a sign of the Holy Spirit, of strength, and of health.

The laying on of hands is a sign of the healing touch of Jesus and the care of God and the Church.

Important symbolic actions and words used during the anointing of the sick can also help us understand the meaning and importance of this sacrament.

  1. The priest visits the sick as sign of the Church's concern for the sick.

  2. The priest places his hands on the head of the sick and anoints the forehead and hands with blessed oil as he prays these words: "Through this holy anointing may the Lord in His love and mercy help you with the grace of the Holy Spirit. May the Lord who frees you from sin save you and raise you up." (Pastoral Care of the Sick: Rites of Anointing and Viaticum)

  3. The priest prays to the risen Jesus. He prays for three things for the sick person: (a) easing of suffering, with strength to cope with it; (b) physical healing of mind and body; and (c) forgiveness of sins.

We Believe

Every human person is a relational being. As human persons, we develop relationships outside of our families. We reach out to others in friendship.

Friendship is a relationship that we should treasure. We share our thoughts, ideas, interests, dreams, secrets, and even problems with our friends. A true friend brings out our best qualities. The qualities we look for in a friend are the same qualities we should be willing to share as a friend.

Friendship forms the basis of all relationships, especially the lifelong relationship between a man and a woman called marriage.

God said that it is not good for man to be alone. So He made a suitable partner for him. This means that man and woman need each other. They need to love, care for, and help one another.

The Bible also says, "That is why a man leaves his father and mother and is united to his wife, and they become one flesh." This means that man and woman were created for one another. They form an unbreakable union of their two lives. (CCC 1605)

In the Church, the love between a man and a woman is blessed. This blessing is given in the sacrament of matrimony. Matrimony is also known as marriage.

Marriage is one of the sacraments of vocation. It is the union of a man and a woman as husband and wife. They trust, love, and believe in each other. They serve each other's needs. They respect and forgive each other. They express their feelings and thoughts to each other lovingly. They are faithful to one another. (CFC 1912)

Married couples should pray for God's guidance to help them overcome marital problems. They should be reminded by their marriage vow or promise that says: "To be of one heart and soul, from this day forward, for better, for worse, for richer, for poorer, in sickness and in health, until death do us part."

The bride and groom themselves are the ministers of the sacrament of matrimony. The priest represents the Church and presides over the marriage ceremony. When contracted in the presence of a priest and two witnesses, the marriage ceremony becomes valid (Canon 1108 § 1).

The following symbols are used in a wedding ceremony to explain the deeper meaning of the sacrament of matrimony:

  1. Rings as symbol of love and fidelity

  2. Lighted candle as symbol of Christ, the Light of the world who will give light to the married life of the couple

  3. Veil as symbol of the holiness of marriage and the commitment and total self-giving of the couple to one another

  4. Cord as symbol of unity and unbreakable bond between the husband and wife

  5. Arrhae as symbol of material possessions and willingness of the husband to provide for the material needs of his wife and children

Rite of the Sacrament of Matrimony

Matrimony is normally celebrated within the Mass, after the reading of the Gospel and the homily and before the Prayer of the Faithful (SC 78).

  1. Introduction. After the homily the priest addresses the couple and requests them to state their intentions publicly.

  2. Statement of Intention. The couple states their intention to be married. They testify that they have come freely and without reservation and that they will love and honor each other for the rest of their lives. They accept the children that God may give them and bring them up according to the law of Christ and the Catholic faith.

  3. Giving of Consent. The priest instructs the bride and groom to join hands and express their total commitment and lifelong fidelity to one another. They exchange marriage vows.

  4. Reception of Consent. The priest, in the name of God and the community, accepts the consent of the couple and blesses their commitment. They are acknowledged or welcomed as husband and wife.

  5. Blessing of Arrhae and Exchange of Rings. The arrhae is blessed and is given by the husband to his wife. The couple exchange rings after these have been blessed as a symbol of their love and fidelity.

  6. Nuptial Blessings. A long nuptial blessing follows after the Lord's Prayer.

Joseph's Dreams

Joseph, a young man of seventeen, was tending the flocks with his brothers, the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives, and he brought their father a hard report about them.

Now Israel loved Joseph more than any of his other sons, because he had been born to him in his old age; and he made an ornate robe for him. When his brothers saw that their father loved him more than any of them, they hated him and could not speak a kind word to him.

Joseph had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers, they hated him all the more. He said to them, “Listen to this dream I had: We were binding sheaves of grain out in the field when suddenly my sheaf rose and stood upright, while your sheaves gathered around mine and bowed down to it.”

His brothers said to him, “Do you intend to reign over us? Will you actually rule us?” And they hated him all the more because of his dream and what he had said.

Then he had another dream, and he told it to his brothers. “Listen,” he said, “I had another dream, and this time the sun and moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me.”

When he told his father as well as his brothers, his father rebuked him and said, “What is this dream you had? Will your mother and I and your brothers actually come and bow down to the ground before you?” His brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the matter in mind. (Genesis 37:2–11)

C. We Believe

The story of Joseph reveals that favoritism and jealousy can cause problems in the relationships in the family. His father's favoritism and his brothers' jealousy brought grief, guilt, suffering, and division in the family.

The same problems threaten the unity and love among family members today. Hence parents and children alike must learn how to communicate with one another openly and honestly in a loving and forgiving atmosphere. They must also be willing to admit mistakes and the whole family must look beyond itself and strive to offer Christian witness through the Gospel values of justice and protection of human rights (CFC 1027). St. Teresa of Calcutta even gave this advice to a married couple struggling in their relationship: “Pray and forgive.” And to young people from violent homes, “I say pray and forgive.” And again even to single mother with no family support: “Pray and forgive.”

As followers of Jesus we have the responsibilities to protect and preserve the sanctity of the family and of marriage. The foundation of the family is marriage. It is the basis for a common life of man, woman, and children. Only in marriage is unconditional reliability guaranteed for its roots, protection, and room for development (DOCAT, 125).

In marriage or matrimony, the married couples are called to commit themselves to form a Christian family where God's love is experienced through the parents and children (GS 48). Parents must work hard to ensure the happiness of the family by loving one another as they take good care of their children. Children, on the other hand, must show love, gratitude, and honor to their parents by respecting, obeying, appreciating, and taking good care of them in their old age.
