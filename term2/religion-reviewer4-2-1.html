Symbols of Christ

During the period when Christians were being persecuted, they used different symbols to represent their faith in Jesus. These symbols can be seen in religious paintings, altars and carvings on Church walls, Mass vestments, sacred linens, and many devotional objects.

The following are four important symbols of Jesus through the years:

No. Name Symbol
1. Alpha and Omega Α Ω
2. Chi-Rho ☧
3. IHS IHS
4. Ichthus (fish) ΙΧΘΥΣ 🐟

1. Alpha and Omega — Α Ω

Α (Alpha) is the first letter of the Greek alphabet, while Ω (Omega) is the last letter. Together, Alpha and Omega represent that Christ is the beginning and the end.

2. Chi-Rho — ☧

Chi-Rho combines two Greek letters: Χ (Chi) and Ρ (Rho). They are the first two letters of the Greek word Christos, meaning Christ.

3. IHS — IHS

IHS is a Christian symbol derived from the first three letters of the Greek name for Jesus (ΙΗΣΟΥΣ). It is commonly used to represent Jesus Christ.

4. Ichthus (Fish) — ΙΧΘΥΣ

Ichthus is the Greek word for fish. The fish became an early Christian symbol. The Greek letters ΙΧΘΥΣ are also understood as an abbreviation for “Jesus Christ, Son of God, Savior.”

Symbols for easy copying:

  • Alpha & Omega: Α Ω

  • Chi-Rho: ☧

  • IHS: IHS

  • Ichthus: 𓆟 / 🐟 or ΙΧΘΥΣ

Christogram

Christogram is a monogram or typographic abbreviation for the name of Jesus Christ, used as a traditional symbol within Christian art and iconography. These visual abbreviations served as early identifiers for believers, confessed the faith during times of persecution, and continue to decorate ecclesiastical architecture, vestments, and devotional objects.

No. Name Meaning
1. Alpha and Omega (ΑΩ) A Christological letter-symbol using the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet to signify Christ as the beginning and the end.
2. Chi-rho (☧) A Christogram formed from the Greek letters Chi (Χ) and Rho (Ρ), the first two letters of ΧΡΙΣΤΟΣ (Christos).
3. IHS A Christogram derived from the first three letters of the Greek name ΙΗΣΟΥΣ (Iēsous).
4. Ichthus (fish) A Christogrammatic acrostic meaning Iēsous Christos Theou Yios Sōtēr (Jesus Christ, Son of God, Savior).
5. INRI It is an acronym for the Latin phrase Iesus Nazarenus, Rex Iudaeorum, which translates to “Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews.”
6. IC XC NIKA A Byzantine Christogram formed from the first and last letters of ΧΡΙΣΤΟΣ, accompanied by NIKA (conquers), proclaiming Jesus Christ conquers.

When we join groups, organizations, clubs, and movements in the school or in the community, we are welcomed through an initiation rite. Initiation is an admission process by which new members are formally accepted as members of the group.

The Catholic Church is a religious group or community. It is God’s family. We are initiated into God’s family through the sacraments of initiation, which include the sacraments of baptism, confirmation, and Holy Eucharist. These sacraments enable us to carry out our mission as Christians in both the Church and the world.

Baptism is the first sacrament of initiation. It brings us new life in Christ and incorporates us into the Church. It means that we become members of the Church and adopted brothers and sisters of Christ (CFC 1647, CCC 1266).

During baptism, the whole community welcomes the new member into the Church. Baptism may be done either by immersion or infusion. Immersion is the act of being completely covered with water. Infusion refers to the pouring of the holy water on the forehead of the person to be baptized. Baptism is carried out by the priest saying, “I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.” The minister of baptism is a bishop, a priest, or a deacon (CCC 1213, CCC 1240).

There are four symbols of baptism. These are water, chrism oil, lighted candle, and white garment.

1. Water is the symbol of the saving presence of God. The pouring of water is a sign of sharing in the new life in Christ.

2. Chrism oil is scented or perfumed oil blessed by the bishop. Anointing during baptism signifies being both healed and strengthened by the Spirit. It is also a special sign of the baptized person’s mission as follower of Jesus.

3. The lighted candle is the symbol of the light and warmth of God’s love that Jesus gives as the Light of the world. As followers of Jesus, we have to let Christ’s light shine for all to see.

4. The white garment represents our new dignity as Christians. White signifies mystery and purity, death, and life.

Gifts of the Spirit

A shoot will come up from the stump of Jesse; from his roots a Branch will bear fruit.

The Spirit of the Lord will rest on him—the Spirit of wisdom and of understanding, the Spirit of counsel and of knowledge and fear of the Lord—and he will delight in the fear of the Lord. (Isaiah 11:1–3)

B. Answer these questions.

1. What are the different gifts of the Holy Spirit?

The seven gifts of the Holy Spirit are wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, fear of the Lord, and piety.

2. To whom do they belong?

They belong to the Holy Spirit, who gives these gifts to us, especially through the Sacrament of Confirmation.

3. Why does the Holy Spirit give us these spiritual gifts?

The Holy Spirit gives us these spiritual gifts to help us grow as mature Christians, live our Christian faith, serve God and others, and become witnesses for Christ in words and deeds.

Lesson Text

In baptism, we were welcomed and became members of God’s family and the Catholic Church. Through this membership, we are given roles and responsibilities to participate in Christ’s mission.

The gifts of the Holy Spirit which we receive in confirmation sustain the moral life of those who receive them.

The Holy Spirit, our Helper and Guide, gives us these spiritual gifts. Through these gifts, we become witnesses for Christ in words and deeds. We become inspired to share and defend our faith. We are able to serve God and others just as Jesus did. We bring light and life to others. We receive the gifts of the Holy Spirit in the sacrament of confirmation (Code of Canon Law 879).

Confirmation is another sacrament of initiation that draws us to a closer union with the Church through the power of the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit gives us the strength to grow as mature Christians and to actively spread the word of God. (CFC 1658)

The sacrament of confirmation is conferred through laying on of hands, anointing the forehead, and saying the prayer:

“Be sealed with the gifts of the Holy Spirit” (CFC 1660).

The minister of confirmation is the bishop. The godparents help the one to be confirmed to grow as a good Catholic Christian.

There are seven special gifts of the Holy Spirit that we receive in confirmation. These are wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, fear of the Lord, and piety. These gifts are virtues waiting to be accepted and developed. These serve as guidelines to Christian maturity. (CCC 1831)

The Seven Gifts of the Holy Spirit

1. Wisdom is the gift which enables us to appreciate spiritual things and to have right judgment in deciding what is true and good.

2. Understanding is the gift which enables us to grasp the meaning of the truths of faith. It helps us act in ways that show we are living our Christian faith.

3. Counsel is the gift which enables us to identify the right course of action in a given situation.

4. Fortitude is the gift which allows us to be strong and brave to stand up for our faith in Christ.

5. Knowledge is the ability to give importance to the value and use of created things.

6. Fear of the Lord is the gift which enables us to show respect and awe toward God. It helps us to thank and praise God for the gift of life.

7. Piety is the gift which teaches us about duty, respect, and loyalty toward our parents, family, and country.

The fruits of the Spirit are signs of the presence of the Holy Spirit in our lives. The tradition of the Church lists twelve of them. These are charity, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, generosity, gentleness, faithfulness, modesty, self-control, and chastity. (CCC 1832)

The two symbols of confirmation are laying on of hands and anointing with chrism.

The laying on of hands is an ancient practice of conferring or giving power to another person. In confirmation, the bishop does the laying on of hands as a sign of sending forth the Holy Spirit and handing us the rights and responsibilities as mature Christians.

The anointing with chrism symbolizes our commitment to profess our faith, practice it boldly, and spread the “aroma of Christ.” (2 Corinthians 2:15)

Jesus Sends Out the Twelve

Jesus called his twelve disciples to him and gave them authority to drive out impure spirits and to heal every disease and sickness.

These are the names of the twelve apostles: first, Simon (who is called Peter) and his brother Andrew; James son of Zebedee, and his brother John; Philip and Bartholomew; Thomas and Matthew the tax collector; James son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus; Simon the Zealot and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him.

Jesus' Instructions to the Twelve

These twelve Jesus sent out with the following instructions:

“Do not go among the Gentiles or enter any town of the Samaritans. Go rather to the lost sheep of Israel. As you go, proclaim this message: ‘The kingdom of heaven has come near.’ Heal the sick, raise the dead, cleanse those who have leprosy, drive out demons. Freely you have received; freely give.

“Do not get any gold or silver or copper to take with you in your belts—no bag for the journey or extra shirt or sandals or a staff, for the worker is worth his keep.”

(Matthew 10:1–10)

Continuing the Mission of Jesus

Jesus called His twelve apostles to carry on His work and to call others to be His disciples. He sent them on a mission to proclaim the Good News of God's Kingdom: “As the Father has sent me, I am sending you” (John 20:21). He gave them the authority to teach, heal, and cast out devils.

Today, the Church, founded by Christ, continued the saving mission of Christ (LG 5). As Christians, by virtue of our baptism, therefore, we share “the obligation of spreading the faith” (LG 17). PCP II asserts: “All are called to mission... all—without exception—are called to evangelize” (PCP II 402, CFC 1414).

The corporal and spiritual works of mercy are lists of actions or ways by which all Catholics can actively continue the mission of Jesus in our lives, schools, parishes, country, and the world.

The Corporal Works of Mercy

  1. Feed the hungry

  2. Give drink to the thirsty

  3. Clothe those who lack clothes

  4. Visit those in prison

  5. Shelter the homeless

  6. Visit the sick

  7. Bury the dead

The Spiritual Works of Mercy

  1. Correct sinners

  2. Teach those who need to learn

  3. Give advice to those who need it

  4. Comfort those who suffer

  5. Be patient with others

  6. Forgive injuries

  7. Pray for the living and the dead
