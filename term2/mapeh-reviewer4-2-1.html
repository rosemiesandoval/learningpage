Understanding the Building Blocks of Music: Rhythm, Beat, Notes, and Rests

Music is all around us, from the songs we hum to the grand orchestras we hear. But what exactly makes music move and resonate? Let us explore some of its fundamental elements.

Rhythm

At its heart, rhythm is the organized pattern of sounds and silences in music. It is what gives music its unique character, making it dynamic and engaging. Think of it as the dance of the sounds, creating a sense of forward motion and flow. When you find yourself clapping along to the lyrics of a song, you are intuitively following one of the many rhythms woven into the piece.

Beat

Now, let us talk about the beat. If rhythm is the dance, the beat is the steady pulse that guides it. Imagine the consistent ticking of a clock or the steady pulse that continues even when you are active. That is the beat—the underlying, unchanging pulse that keeps all the musical elements in sync. It is what you naturally tap your foot or clap along with when listening to music.

For example, if you are holding a tambourine while your favorite song plays, every time you make it in time with the music is following the beat. The beat provides the foundational tempo, or how fast or slow the music is, to which all other musical parts are built.

To reiterate, while rhythm creates varied and expressive patterns through different sounds, durations, and silences, the beat remains the constant, steady pulse that anchors the music. Rhythm is dynamic and can change to create different emotions and ideas, but the beat provides the consistent framework.

For a deeper understanding of the distinct differences between rhythm and beat, watch the video “Primary Music Lesson – Beat vs. Rhythm.”

Notes and Rests

To create music, we use fundamental symbols called notes and rests. Notes are the visual representations of sounds in music. Think of them as the “dots” and lines you use to draw a picture. Each note symbol tells a musician how high or low a sound should be and, crucially, how long to hold that sound. Think of them as instructions for singing or playing an instrument.

Each note symbol has a specific duration, measured in beats. In many common songs, we often count the quarter note as one beat.

Rests, on the other hand, represent silence in music. They are just as crucial as notes! Just like notes, rests have specific durations, also measured in beats. A short rest means a quick pause, while a long rest means a longer silence. Rests are essential because they give music breathing room, making it more interesting and allowing the sounds to stand out clearly.

Here are the common note and rest symbols and their typical beat values, particularly in music with 2/4 and 4/4 time signatures:

Note Symbol Number of Beats Meaning Equivalent Rest
Whole Note Four This note holds a long and sustained sound. Imagine stretching out a word for a full four counts. Whole Rest
Half Note Two This note is half as long as a whole note. Two half notes fit into the space of one whole note. Half Rest
Quarter Note One This holds the basic beat. Often, this is the beat you tap your foot to in a song. Quarter Rest
Eighth Note One-half This note is held for half a beat. That means two eighth notes fit into the space of one quarter note, making them sound quick and flowing. Eighth Rest
Sixteenth Note One-fourth Four sixteenth notes fit into the space of one quarter note, making them sound very fast and short. Sixteenth Rest

When you combine notes and rests in various sequences, you can construct an endless variety of musical pieces, from the simple melodies you sing in school to the rich, complex music performed at vibrant festivals across the Philippines. It is like drawing an intricate picture, but with sounds and silences as your art tools!

Exploring Tempo and Musical Traditions

Tempo

Imagine you are tapping your feet to a song. Sometimes, you tap slowly because the music feels calm and deliberate. Other times, you tap quickly because the music is fast and energetic. This "speed" of the music—how fast or slow it goes—is called tempo. Tempo helps to set the mood and energy of a musical piece.

Music composers often use Italian words to indicate tempo. Here are some of the most commonly used tempo markings:

  • Lento – Slowly

  • Largo – Slow and stately (often implying a broad, dignified pace)

  • Adagio – Leisurely (a comfortable, slow tempo)

  • Andante – At a walking pace (a comfortable, slow tempo)

  • Allegro – Fast (often cheerful)

  • Vivace – Lively (even more energetic than allegro)

  • Presto – Very quickly (as fast as possible)

Liturgical and Devotional Music in the Philippines

Rhythm and tempo are especially evident in liturgical and devotional music, which holds a very special place in the Philippines. These are spiritual songs used in churches and during times of prayer, designed to foster a deeper connection to the divine and unite communities. The rhythms and tempos in this type of music often change depending on the liturgical season. For example, the music can be fast and festive during Christmas, contrasting with the more solemn and contemplative rhythms of Holy Week.

Liturgical music is specifically created for use in church services, such as Mass. Songs are sung during the Mass to help people pray, participate, and celebrate their faith together. Used within church services, this type of music often incorporates a steady, meditative beat that guides the flow of the service.

The rhythm in liturgical music is generally moderate and repetitive, which helps to create a reverent and reflective atmosphere during prayer and worship. The tempo of these songs can vary—it can be fast, slow, or moderately paced—depending on the specific part of the service and the intended arrangement.

Devotional music helps individuals express their love and devotion, particularly in religious contexts. Unlike liturgical music, these pieces are not strictly produced for formal liturgical use, meaning they can be performed and enjoyed outside of church services. This flexibility allows devotional music to be less bound by strict liturgical traditions. Musicians and singers of devotional music often use a wider range of instruments and vocal tones to express their profound love for God.

Two excellent Filipino examples of devotional music are the pastores a belen and the pasyon, often performed during Christmas and Holy Week, respectively.

  • Pastores a Belen – This is a traditional Filipino Christmas performance, introduced by Spanish priests in the 1800s. It beautifully recounts the story of the shepherds' journey to Bethlehem to see the baby Jesus. Popular in the Bicol region, the pastores features a lively, upbeat rhythm. Its cheerful and syncopated rhythmic pattern perfectly matches the festive spirit of the shepherds' journey, often performed with colorful and elaborate costumes and dance movements. This cherished tradition is a special part of Christmas in the Philippines, helping communities remember the story of the first Christmas through a beautiful blend of singing, dancing, and storytelling that brings everyone together during the holiday season.

You can find many performances of pastores a belen music online to experience its joyous spirit.

  • Pasyon – A vital tradition in the Philippines during Holy Week, the pasyon is an epic narrative that tells the story of Jesus Christ’s life, suffering, death, and resurrection. The first printed version of the pasyon was attributed to Gaspar Aquino de Belen. The story is sung in a special, gradual, and often chant-like tune. It features a slow, deliberate rhythmic pattern, which creates a solemn and reflective tone. The beat is steady but extended, allowing the profound story of Christ’s life and sacrifice to unfold through a reflective chant. When people sing the pasyon, they often gather in homes or special community spaces, taking turns to sing different parts of the extensive story. This can continue for several days, from morning until night, serving as a powerful way for communities to come together, reflect on Jesus’ story, and express their deep faith.

While the basic tune often remains slow and reflective, these instruments can add more variety and depth to the music. The absence of illness does not mean you are healthy. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), health is a condition of total physical, mental, and social well-being. On the other hand, wellness is defined as having good physical condition through a healthy lifestyle. It also means achieving the best level of health from having a positive view about life.

Personal Health

The ability to actively choose healthy living and be in control of one’s own health is called personal health. This includes one’s emotional, intellectual, social, economic, spiritual, and other aspects of well-being in addition to physical health. To achieve good personal health, one should have a balanced relationship of these aspects.

The two main goals of personal health are to maintain good physical health and to keep one’s living and working environments germ-free. Keeping oneself clean can help prevent illnesses and the spread of germs. It will also keep the body in good shape and give the person more confidence. Being well and looking good makes you feel good.

A person's overall well-being can be influenced by five dimensions of health. The best level of health can be influenced by the balance of these dimensions.

  • Physical health is a condition of the body that includes the size, structure, organs, and body processes and functions. Practicing good personal hygiene, maintaining a healthy diet, exercising daily, and having enough sleep benefit physical health.

  • Mental health is the ability to think and to understand and process information. Mental activities include doing homework, solving math problems, calculating expenses, and answering tests and quizzes.

  • Emotional health is the ability to show and control feelings, such as being sad, happy, angry, and scared, among others. Manifestations of emotional health include smiling at compliments, controlling rage, and expressing emotions through the face.

  • Social health is the ability to adjust to different situations and behaviors. It also pertains to how a person interacts or communicates with other people. Activities related to this are playing outdoor games, communicating with other people, and keeping commitments in studies or work.

  • Spiritual health is one’s faith and beliefs in following practices related to seeking a purpose in life. Visiting places of worship, practicing religious obligations, praying, and having inner peace can help develop spiritual health.

Certain activities often develop different dimensions of health, especially those that are closely related with each other. For example, treating anxiety may fall under mental health and emotional health, depending on the cause of one’s anxiety. Volunteer work may enrich one’s social health and spiritual health. Even so, working out may not only develop physical health but social health, too, as a person may want to seek comfort in a new set of friends who are into sports.

Importance of Personal Health for Active Living

One secret to good health is to practice a variety of physical activities and to maintain correct personal cleanliness. If you do these regularly, you can avoid having illnesses.

For one, you can be healthier if you follow good personal hygiene, or the practice of keeping oneself clean. Washing your hands after handling dirty materials is an example of good personal hygiene.

If you want to lead an active and healthy life, you must be aware of how your body should be cleaned, as well as what things you need to do to keep your body functioning properly. The following are some reasons why personal health is important for active living:

1. Maintaining proper personal hygiene can project a positive body image and a positive impression. This may result in improved confidence and self-esteem (how one feels about oneself). Someone who smells bad and appears dirty is normally seen as unattractive and possibly even dangerous to others.

2. Having a healthy body allows the performance of more physical activities that can help one move more and avoid being inactive. One can enjoy group activities with friends and family if one's body is healthy and strong.

3. Having good personal health lets one enjoy life for a longer period.

Hygiene is a set of practices or tasks to keep oneself clean. Personal hygiene involves practices for grooming and taking care of oneself.

We must practice personal hygiene practices and participate in health checkups. Good hygiene practices help prevent or minimize the spread of diseases. Some hygiene practices that you can do on your own are the following.

Hand Washing

One of the best ways to protect yourself from getting diseases is to wash your hands. You use your hands in almost every activity. That means they are in contact with different things. Washing hands frequently can assure you that germs are eliminated so they do not get to other parts of your body. It is best to wash hands in these situations:

Before and After After
Preparing food Using the toilet
Eating a meal Getting soiled hands
Tending a sick person Sneezing, coughing, or blowing the nose
Treating a cut wound Touching animals or pets
Touching dirty surfaces or trash

Here are the steps on how to wash your hands the right way:

  1. Wet your hands with clean water.

  2. Use soap and create a lather by rubbing your hands together.

  3. Rub your palms, backs of your hands, spaces between fingers, and undersides of fingernails. Scrub your hands for 20 seconds, which is about the same time you sing "Happy Birthday" twice.

  4. Rinse your hands completely with water.

  5. Pat your hands dry.

Fingernail Hygiene

As you use your hands with anything, fingernails can easily collect dirt, where germs reside. Unclean fingernails also spread these germs. Thus, it is important to clip your fingernails short. When washing hands, the undersides of fingernails should not be missed. The same goes with toenails.

It is also important to thoroughly clean tools used in cleaning fingernails, such as nail cutter (nail clippers) and nail file. To prevent fingernail infections and stop the spread of germs, the following should be observed:

  1. Keep your fingernails short and clip them frequently.

  2. Use a nail brush to scrub the undersides of your fingernails.

  3. Clean fingernail tools before and after using.

  4. Do not bite your fingernails.

Facial Hygiene

Keeping your face clean is important for your overall health. As much as possible, avoid touching your face with dirty hands. If you really have to, it is important to wash your hands with soap and clean water. Then dry your hands and face with a fresh towel or piece of tissue paper. Use another towel or tissue paper to clean your nose and ears.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, the virus was easily transferred when contaminated hands touched one's eyes, nose, or mouth. It was advised then to always wash the hands with soap and water or to use alcohol to disinfect the hands.

Coughing and Sneezing Hygiene

You can prevent the spread of bacteria and viruses by coughing and sneezing onto your inner elbow or covering your mouth and nose with clean tissue or handkerchief. Make sure to wash your hands with soap and water or use alcohol after coughing or sneezing on them. Properly dispose of any used tissues, too.

Wearing a face mask has been the norm for people who are suffering from coughing. The face mask acts as a barrier against droplets containing bacteria or viruses from spreading to the air when one coughs or sneezes.

Foot Hygiene

Properly washing your feet with soap and clean water can help maintain clean feet and avoid foot problems. One problem is having smelly feet. This happens when sweat is trapped in your socks or shoes. Bacteria and fungi thrive in moist areas of the feet. So it is important to always wash your feet. Also, make sure to dry your feet first before putting on clean socks.

Examine your feet for wounds, sores, and infections. Make sure to treat them before wearing any footwear. Avoid walking barefoot as much as possible. But if you have to, like after walking on the sand or grass, be sure to wash your feet afterward.

Hair and Scalp Hygiene

Keep your hair and scalp healthy by practicing hair care routines including taking a bath every day, washing your hair with shampoo, and rinsing it with clean water. Wash your hair often to remove dirt and oil from your scalp. Comb it to untangle the hair strands. Avoid sharing your comb, hair clips, or bath towel to avoid spreading hair and scalp infections.

Genital Hygiene

The genital area refers to the body organ where you pee (or urinate). There is a different organ for boys and for girls. The basic hygiene for genitals is to wash them with gentle soap, which is normally done when taking a bath. Be sure to pat the area dry using a clean towel.

To avoid getting irritations or infections in the area, always wear clean underwear, preferably of cotton material because it absorbs sweat easily. Also, wear loose clothing to allow air to circulate in the lower parts of your body.

It is important to pee once you feel like it and avoid holding it back. This is to avoid getting infections.

Oral Hygiene

Your teeth become easily visible when you smile, while your tongue is responsible for tasting the food you eat. To clean your teeth, always brush them at least two times a day. Use a toothpaste of your choice. When brushing your teeth, make short strokes and circular motions. Make sure also to cover all the teeth. Good oral hygiene prevents a buildup of materials in the teeth that cause tooth decay.

Do not forget to brush the tongue, too. This helps remove bacteria and prevent bad breath. Stick out your tongue and scrape it starting at the back toward the front. Toothbrushes should also be replaced after three to four months.

Some use floss, a flat thread-like material, to remove materials between the teeth. Finally, visit the dentist (tooth doctor) for dental exams one to two times a year.

Ear Hygiene

Everyone occasionally develops an accumulation of earwax. Some might be found outside the ear canal. Use a clean, damp washcloth to clean the outside. Hardened earwax should be removed by a doctor or trained health worker. Experts advise not to insert anything into your ear canal. Earwax is naturally occurring and functions to trap dirt entering the ear. Using a cotton bud might only push the earwax further into the ear canal. Only wipe the dried earwax that is expelled out of the ear canal.

Clothes Hygiene

It is essential to change your clothes every day or as soon as they get dirty. The inner layer of clothing, such as underwear and undershirt, collects sweat and dirt from our skin. The outer layer of clothing is prone to stains and outside dirt. These clothes should be regularly washed using detergent (laundry soap) to remove germs and dirt. They should likewise be rinsed thoroughly to remove soap particles that might irritate the skin.

Current Health Status and Body Awareness

You might be feeling well at first glance, but how do you really know if your body is functioning well? This is the role of health screening and assessment, which are performed by doctors and medical professionals. You can only know your health status by undergoing these assessments.

Common health assessments include measuring one’s height and weight, testing one’s hearing and vision, and checking one’s dental health. Through these assessments, people can learn about their current state of health. The doctors will also advise what foods to eat and what physical activities to engage in to stay healthy.

Health Appraisals or Screening Tests

It is important to have your body checked regularly. Your eyes and ears should also be checked regularly as you grow to ensure that your senses of sight and hearing are in the best state. The same goes with your teeth to prevent mouth problems. Health appraisals are done by doctors and health professionals.

The following are some appraisals or screening tests that you can go through.

1. Medical Examination

This generally keeps you updated on your overall state of health. Your overall health is assessed by a physician (other term for doctor) during an examination of the basic functioning of your organs, management of any problems, updating of your immunizations, and maintenance of your health. A physician will usually recommend more screening tests if a suspected medical condition is found.

This helps for the detection of communicable diseases such as chickenpox, common cold, influenza (flu), measles, and COVID-19. Other conditions that can be discovered are high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, arthritis, digestive problems, allergies, heart disease, and lung disease.

2. Dental Examination

This keeps you updated on any dental issues. During a dental exam, the dentist will look for tooth cavities and gum diseases. The dentist will also check your face, neck, and mouth for any unusual characteristics and determine the likelihood of you experiencing further problems with your oral health. A dental examination may also include dental radiograph (dental X-ray) to help the dentist detect tooth and gum issues. Also during a dental exam, the dentist will likely discuss your eating habits and oral hygiene routine, such as proper brushing and using of a floss.

3. Height Taking

This provides an estimation of your growth rate. In clinics and hospitals, a stadiometer is used to measure one’s height. To use it, remove your shoes, stand up on straight legs, put your arms down at your sides, and level your shoulders. Stare straight ahead. The nurse will take your height by adjusting the slider to touch the top of your head. Height is usually expressed in centimeters or feet and inches.

4. Weight Taking

This lets you know if you are within a healthy weight range. Weight monitoring lets you keep track of your growth and development. In clinics and hospitals, a more accurate weighing scale is used. To use it, remove your shoes and heavy clothing, stand straight with feet slightly apart, and avoid making any movement. The nurse will record your weight. Weight is expressed in kilograms or sometimes in pounds.

5. Vision Test

This assesses your vision and how clear you see objects. A Snellen chart is used to test your vision. This chart usually consists of letters arranged from biggest to smallest. An ophthalmologist or an optometrist will have you read the series of letters of the chart from 20 feet away. You will be given a score separately for your left and right eye. If you have difficulty seeing clearly, you may be prescribed to wear eyeglasses.

6. Scoliosis Test

This aims to check for any abnormal curving of your spine. Scoliosis is the condition that causes the spine to curve irregularly to one side. If the condition is addressed before the bones fully develop, it is treatable. This is why it is important that your spine is checked at an early age. The following are visible signs of having scoliosis:

  • One shoulder looks higher than the other.

  • The head is not aligned to the center of the body.

  • One side of the hips appears higher or is sticking out.

  • One side of the rib cage looks higher than the other side when bending forward.

  • The waist is uneven.

  • The entire body is leaning to one side.

7. Hearing Test

This lets you determine whether you are experiencing hearing loss. There are various types of hearing tests that detect hearing loss. One such test is audiometry, where you wear headphones and you indicate every time you hear a tone. An ENT (“ear-nose-throat” doctor) and an audiologist (hearing specialist) can conduct a hearing test.

Corrective Action

Various screening tests allow doctors to identify health concerns that you may have. After you have determined a health concern, a corrective action is needed. It consists of steps done to address health issues. Following it aims to improve your health by suggesting you to change lifestyle choices or habits.

Based on the results of medical examinations and tests, corrective actions are meant to lead you to have a healthier lifestyle. You may be asked to consume more nutritious meals, drink enough water, avoid sugary drinks, exercise frequently or be physically active, get enough rest, and reduce screen time and sitting. Getting regular checkups is a common practice to monitor the progress of your health.

Depending on the condition of your teeth, a dentist may recommend extracting (pagbunot) a damaged or decaying tooth. A tooth filling is done if the dentist thinks that a crack, chip, or minor cavity in a tooth can be repaired by filling it with a material. Most of the time, a dentist performs tooth cleaning by removing materials and stains on your teeth using special dental tools.

Your height and weight measurements are used to calculate your body mass index (BMI). It measures your height in relation to your weight to determine if you are normal, underweight, overweight, or obese (extremely fat). This classification allows a physician to assess your risk for other diseases as you grow older. Some of these diseases may include hypertension (high blood pressure), diabetes, and cancer. In the meantime, taking your height and weight measurements helps in identifying any growth problem of a child like you.

If you are proven to have vision problems following a vision test, you may be prescribed to wear eyeglasses or contact lenses to correct your existing vision. Wearing such things allows you to have clearer vision. You may not be able to tell right away that yourself if your vision is blurred unless you have a vision test. Also, you may be told to have your eyes rest since constant exposure to digital screens (from cellphones, tablets, television, and gadgets) can strain them.

In many cases, minor scoliosis may be managed with exercise, medical supervision, and physical therapy. In mild cases, a scoliosis brace is used to stop the development of further curving of the spine. A brace is a hard plastic jacket worn over the upper body. Surgery is only needed for severe cases.

Hearing loss may make everyday activities difficult, such as taking part in conversations and listening to instructions. Using a hearing aid may be recommended. A hearing aid makes sounds louder for its wearer. It is best to prevent developing hearing loss by taking care of your ears. When listening to music, it is a good idea to turn down the volume, especially if you use earphones. Also, avoid inserting anything into your ear canal.

After every screening test, be sure to follow the advice of your doctor. If you do this, you will have improved results the next time you go for a screening test.
