📚 Chapter Summary: The perimeter is the distance around a polygon — the sum of the lengths of all its sides.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Shapes with equal sides use a multiplication shortcut: Equilateral triangle = 3 × side, Square/Rhombus = 4 × side. Shapes with different sides just get added up: Rectangle = 2 × (L + W), Kite = 2L + 2S, and general quadrilaterals/trapezoids = add all 4 sides!

📚 Chapter Summary: Perimeter word problems often ask you to find a missing side, compare two shapes, or calculate leftover material.
🧮 Problem-Solving Steps

🧠 MEMORY TRICK If you know the perimeter but need a missing side, just flip the formula around: instead of P = 2(L+W), solve W = (P ÷ 2) − L. Same idea works for any shape!
✅ True or False — Key Reminders
FALSEAn obtuse triangle can be a right triangle → a triangle's angles add up to 180°, so it can't have both an obtuse and a right angle.
FALSEA quadrilateral is always a parallelogram → only quadrilaterals with two pairs of parallel sides are parallelograms.
FALSEA rhombus is always a square → a rhombus only needs 4 equal sides, not 4 right angles.
FALSEA trapezoid is a parallelogram → it only has one pair of parallel sides, not two.
FALSEA right angle measures 180° → a right angle is exactly 90°; 180° is a straight angle.
FALSEThe formula for a rectangle's perimeter is P = L × W → that's the formula for AREA; perimeter is P = 2(L + W).

Measuring Angles

The measure of an angle is the amount of opening of the sides in the angle. A protractor is a device that is used to measure an angle.

Example 1:

To measure an angle, place the protractor on one side of the angle, with the vertex at the 0-point. Then, follow the markings on the protractor from 0 to the other side of the angle. In the figure on the right, the other side of the angle is at 90°. So, the angle measures 90 degrees (90°).

Example 2:

Once again, measure the angle by placing the protractor on one side of the angle with the vertex at the 0-point. Follow the markings on the protractor from 0 to the other side of the angle. Determine the measure of the angle by looking at the other side of the angle. Here, the other side of the angle is at 60°. So, the angle measures 60 degrees (60°).

Example 3:

For the last example, again position the 0-point of the protractor at the vertex. Then follow the markings on the protractor from 0 to the other side of the angle. In this example, the other side of the angle is at 140°. So, the angle measures 140 degrees (140°).

Drawing Angles

To draw an angle, we use a protractor and a ruler. Study the following example:

Example: Draw an angle that measures 50°.

Step 1: Draw a straight line using a ruler.

Step 2: Place the center of the protractor at one end of the line. This will become the vertex of the angle. Make sure the line is on the baseline of the protractor.

Step 3: Locate 50° on the protractor. There are two sets of numbers on the protractor: the inner numbers and the outer numbers. Look for the zero that is on the same side as the line. It is on the left-hand side and on the outside. Make a mark where 50° is on the outside scale.

Step 4: Remove the protractor. Using a ruler, connect the mark you made to the end of the line that was in the center of the protractor.

Quadrilaterals

A four-sided polygon is called a quadrilateral. Some quadrilaterals have two pairs of parallel sides, and others have only one or no pair of parallel sides.

Classification of Quadrilaterals

Quadrilaterals can be classified as general quadrilateral, parallelogram, trapezoid, or kite.

  • A general quadrilateral has no pairs of parallel sides.

  • A trapezoid is a quadrilateral with only one pair of parallel sides.

  • A parallelogram can be classified as a rectangle or a rhombus.

  • A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two pairs of opposite sides that are both parallel and of equal measure.

  • A kite is a quadrilateral with two pairs of consecutive sides with the same measure.

A rectangle is a parallelogram with four right angles.

A rhombus is a parallelogram with four sides of the same measure.

A special rectangle with four sides of the same measure is a square.

A triangle is a polygon with the least number of sides.

Perimeter of a Polygon

The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of the measures of its sides.

Specific formulas are used to find the perimeters of some polygons.

Perimeter of an Equilateral Triangle

The perimeter of an equilateral triangle can be found using the formula:

P = 3 × a

where a is the measure of its side.

Example

Find the perimeter of an equilateral triangle whose side measures 12 cm.

Solution:

Here, a = 12 cm

Therefore,

P = 3 × a

= 3 × 12 cm

= 36 cm

Answer: 36 cm

Perimeter of a Rectangle

The perimeter of a rectangle can be found using the formula:

P = (2 × L) + (2 × W)

or

P = 2 × (L + W)

Example:

Find the perimeter of the rectangle shown below.

Length = 10
Width = 5

Solution:

To find the perimeter, substitute the length, L = 10, and the width, W = 5, in the formula.

Thus, the perimeter of the rectangle is:

P = 2 × (L + W)

= 2 × (10 + 5)

= 2 × 15

= 30

Since there is no unit indicated, P = 30 units.

Perimeter of a Square and Rhombus

The perimeter of a square is computed using the formula:

P = 4 × a,

where a is the measure of its side.

The same formula can be used to find the perimeter of a rhombus because a rhombus also has four equal sides.

Example:

Find the perimeter of the rhombus shown below.

Each side = 8 cm

Solution:

The perimeter of the rhombus can be found using the formula:

P = 4 × a

The length of a side is a = 8 cm.

P = 4 × a

= 4 × 8 cm

= 32 cm

Perimeter of a General Quadrilateral

In finding the perimeter of a general quadrilateral, all you need to do is add all the lengths of its sides. You can use the formula:

P = a + b + c + d,

where a, b, c, and d are the measures of its sides.

Example:

Find the perimeter of the quadrilateral shown below.

The sides are:

  • 4 cm

  • 5 cm

  • 7 cm

  • 9 cm

Solution:

The lengths of the sides of the given quadrilateral are:

a = 4 cm; b = 5 cm; c = 7 cm; and d = 9 cm.

P = a + b + c + d

P = 4 cm + 5 cm + 7 cm + 9 cm

P = 25 cm

Perimeter of a Trapezoid

In finding the perimeter of a trapezoid, all you need to do is add all the lengths of the sides of it. You can use the formula:

P = a + b + c + d,

where a, b, c, and d are the measures of its sides.

Example:

Find the perimeter of the trapezoid shown below.

The sides are:

  • 3 cm

  • 4 cm

  • 5 cm

  • 8 cm

Solution:

The lengths of the sides of the given trapezoid are:

a = 3 cm; b = 4 cm; c = 5 cm; and d = 8 cm.

P = a + b + c + d

P = 3 cm + 4 cm + 5 cm + 8 cm

P = 20 cm

Perimeter of a Kite

Recall that a kite is a quadrilateral with two pairs of consecutive sides that are equal.

In the figure, there are two longer sides (L) and two shorter sides (S).

To find the perimeter of a kite, use the formula:

P = (2 × L) + (2 × S),

where:

P = perimeter

L = length of the longer side

S = length of the shorter side

Example:

Find the perimeter of the kite shown below.

The longer side = 12 cm
The shorter side = 7 cm

Solution:

The figure shows that the length of the longer side is 12 cm and the length of the shorter side is 7 cm. Then, L = 12 cm and S = 7 cm. The perimeter of the kite is computed as follows:

P = (2 × L) + (2 × S)

P = (2 × 12 cm) + (2 × 7 cm)

P = 24 cm + 14 cm

P = 38 cm

Perimeter of a Composite Figure

A composite figure is made up of triangles, squares, rectangles, and other plane figures. Putting two rectangles or a rectangle and a triangle together forms composite figures. To find the perimeter of a composite figure, find the distance around the figure.

Example: Find the perimeter of the composite figure shown below.

Solution:

Notice from the figure that there are two sides with unknown measures. You have to find the lengths of these sides first before you can determine the perimeter.

To find the length of side A, look at the two rectangles that make up the figure. The opposite sides of the rectangles are of the same length. Thus,

A = 5 cm − 3 cm = 2 cm.

To find the length of side B, look at the two rectangles that make up the figure. The opposite sides of the rectangles are of the same length. Thus,

B = 8 cm − 4 cm = 4 cm.

The perimeter of the composite figure is the sum of the lengths of the sides. Therefore, the perimeter is:

P = 3 cm + 8 cm + 5 cm + 4 cm + 2 cm + 4 cm

= 26 cm

B. Solve the following problems:

1.

Which has a greater perimeter, a square whose side is 7 meters or a rectangle whose length is 9 meters and whose width is 5 meters?

2.

Willy wants to fence his 12-meter by 8-meter vegetable garden, leaving an opening of about 1 meter for a gate. How many meters of fencing wire must he buy?

3.

I have a 9-meter lace, which I will sew around a square tablecloth. One side of the tablecloth measures 2 meters. Do I have enough lace to use? Explain.

4.

A roll of barbed wire has a length of 200 meters. How many meters will be left after fencing a rectangular lot that is 50 meters long and 40 meters wide?

5.

B. Solutions

1. Square vs. Rectangle

Square:

P = 4S
P = 4(7)
P = 28 m

Rectangle:

P = 2(L + W)
P = 2(9 + 5)
P = 2(14)
P = 28 m

Therefore, both have the same perimeter.

✅ Answer: Neither has a greater perimeter. Both have a perimeter of 28 meters.

2. Vegetable Garden

Length = 12 m
Width = 8 m

First, find the perimeter:

P = 2(L + W)

P = 2(12 + 8)
P = 2(20)
P = 40 m

There is a 1-meter opening for the gate:

40 − 1 = 39 m

✅ Answer: Willy must buy 39 meters of fencing wire.

3. Lace Around the Square Tablecloth

Side = 2 m

Perimeter of the square:

P = 4S
P = 4(2)
P = 8 m

He has 9 m of lace.

9 − 8 = 1 m

✅ Answer: Yes, he has enough lace. He needs 8 meters and has 9 meters, so 1 meter will be left.

4. Barbed Wire

Length of wire = 200 m

Lot:

  • Length = 50 m

  • Width = 40 m

Perimeter:

P = 2(L + W)

P = 2(50 + 40)
P = 2(90)
P = 180 m

Wire remaining:

200 − 180 = 20 m

✅ Answer: 20 meters of barbed wire will be left.

5. Grandma's Farm

Perimeter = 980 m

Known sides:

  • 400 m

  • 260 m

  • 150 m

  • Fourth side = ?

Set up the equation:

980 = 400 + 260 + 150 + x

Add the known sides:

400 + 260 + 150 = 810

Therefore:

x = 980 − 810

x = 170 m

✅ Answer: The fourth side is 170 meters.
