📚 Chapter Summary: A complex sentence joins a complete idea with an incomplete idea, using a linking word called a subordinator.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember the formula: "Main + Subordinator + Support" — or flip it: "Subordinator + Support, Main" (with a comma). Either order works, as long as the subordinator connects the two!
🔗 Common Subordinators
Timebefore, while, since, when, whenever
Reasonbecause, since, as
Contrastalthough, even though
REMEMBER Both parts of a complex sentence have a subject and a predicate — but only the main part can stand alone as its own complete sentence.

Count Nouns and Mass Nouns

Nouns can also be classified as either a count noun or a mass noun. A count noun, as the name implies, can be counted. A mass noun is something that cannot be counted or is difficult to count. Study some examples:

Count Nouns

  • typhoon

  • house

  • tank

  • appliance

  • window

  • area

  • device

  • cash

  • book

  • bag

  • powder

  • ice cream

  • clay

  • document

  • mug

  • jewelry

  • magazine

Mass Nouns

  • information

  • electricity

  • nature

  • life

  • rice

  • water

  • rain

Signal Words of Time Order

Read the following events in the story. Take note of the signal words used at the beginning of each sentence.

  • First, Angie's mother called her for lunch and told her to wash her hands. Angie didn't pay attention and started eating.

  • Next, Angie told her mom that she had a stomachache.

  • Then, they went to the doctor. The doctor told Angie that she was infected by microbes.

  • After that, the doctor told Angie ways to stay healthy and prevent diseases.

  • Finally, Angie followed the doctor's advice and she became a healthy child.

The words first, next, then, after that, and finally are some signal words of time order. They are used to introduce steps in a process or sequence of events in a story. Understanding signal words of time order can help you understand the structure of a text.

Remember

  • Reading texts helps activate your schema.

  • Signal words of time order are helpful to use in sequencing a story or showing steps in a process.

Complex Sentences

Most sentences usually contain one complete idea, such as this:

We will support what you want to study.

There are sentences that may have more ideas to express. A sentence containing a complete idea connected with an incomplete idea is called a complex sentence. Compare the next sentence with the first one.

We will support what you want to study when you grow up.

In the second sentence, you have two ideas. The underlined is a complete idea, while the doubly underlined is not complete on its own but merely supports the main idea.

Notice that the second part starts with a subordinator (when). A subordinator connects the two unequal ideas, meaning the complete idea and the incomplete idea. Notice also that both ideas have a subject and a predicate.

Examples:

Since she has learned of many unusual words, Susan now uses them when writing.

She suddenly feels scared to speak her mind now that her parents have so much to say about her future.

Build New Knowledge

  • A complex sentence has a main, complete part and a supporting, incomplete part.

  • A subordinator links these two parts together.

  • Subordinator: while

  • Main idea: Happiness was written on their faces.

  • Supporting idea: as they talked about May’s performance in the school play yesterday.

  • Subordinator: as

  • Main idea: Her father was amazed.

  • Supporting idea: because she did extremely well to portray the character.

Main idea: Her mother was proud of her.

Supporting idea: to get a main role.

Subordinator: to

Quick reminder

A complex sentence has:

Main/complete idea + subordinator + supporting/incomplete idea

Examples:

  • I stayed home because I was sick.

  • Although it was raining, we went outside.

  • She studied hard so that she could pass the exam.

The words because, although, while, when, whenever, since, before, as, even though are examples of subordinators.
