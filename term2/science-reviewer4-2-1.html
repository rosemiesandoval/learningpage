📖 The Human Body — Notes

📚 Chapter Summary: The body is built in four levels, smallest to largest: cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs form organ systems. There are ten organ systems in total, each with its own job.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember the order with: "Cows Trot Over Streams" — Cells → Tissues → Organs → Systems. Each level is a team made of smaller teams!
LevelWhat It Is
CellSmallest living unit — the building block of the body
TissueCells of the same kind working together (e.g. bone tissue, muscle tissue)
OrganDifferent tissues working together (e.g. brain, heart, lungs)
Organ SystemDifferent organs working together for a major function
🗂️ The 10 Organ Systems, by Job
Support & MovementSkeletal, Muscular
Supply & TransportDigestive, Circulatory, Respiratory, Excretory
Control & SensingNervous, Endocrine
Protection & SensationIntegumentary
ReproductionReproductive
📚 Chapter Summary: The skeletal system supports the body, gives it structure, protects organs, and works with joints, ligaments, tendons, and cartilage to enable movement.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Tell ligaments and tendons apart: "Ligaments Link bone to bone, Tendons Tie muscle to bone." Both start with the same letter as their job!
Bone ShapeJobExamples
LongSupport weight, allow movementFemur, phalanges, ulna & radius
FlatProtect & support organsRibs, breastbone, skull bones
ShortSupport weight, small movementsTarsals (ankle), carpals (wrist)
IrregularSpecialized shapesVertebra, ear's hammer/anvil/stirrup
🦴 Axial vs. Appendicular Skeleton
Axial SkeletonSkull, vertebral column (backbone), and thoracic cage (12 pairs of ribs + sternum)
Appendicular SkeletonUpper extremities (shoulder girdle + arms) and lower extremities (pelvic girdle + legs)
JointsFixed (no movement, e.g. cranium) or movable (e.g. hips, knees, fingers)
LigamentConnects bone to bone at a joint
TendonConnects muscle to bone
CartilageCushions joints and acts as a shock absorber
📚 Chapter Summary: The muscular system works with the skeletal system to enable movement, give the body shape, and generate heat. There are three types of muscle: skeletal, smooth, and cardiac.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember the 3 muscle types with: "Skeletons Squeeze Constantly" — Skeletal (voluntary, attached to bone), Smooth (involuntary, in organs), Cardiac (only in the heart, never stops).
Muscle TypeWhere FoundVoluntary?
SkeletalFace, neck, arms, legs, abdomen, chestYes — you control it
SmoothStomach and intestine wallsNo — automatic
CardiacOnly the heartNo — works nonstop
REMEMBER Muscles work in pairs: an extensor straightens a joint, a flexor bends it. The Achilles tendon joins your calf muscle to your heel!
📚 Chapter Summary: The integumentary system (skin, hair, nails, and glands) protects the body, regulates temperature, and is made of three layers: the epidermis, dermis, and hypodermis.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember the skin layers outside-in with: "Every Dog Hides" — Epidermis (top), Dermis (middle), Hypodermis (bottom, fatty layer).
🧴 The 3 Layers of Skin
EpidermisSurface layer; produces melanin, the pigment that gives skin its color and protects from sunburn
DermisMiddle layer; contains sweat glands, hair follicles, and oil glands
HypodermisDeepest layer; fatty tissue that pads organs and keeps heat in, contains elastin
📚 Chapter Summary: The digestive system breaks down food into nutrients through the alimentary canal (mouth to large intestine), aided by accessory organs like the liver, pancreas, and gall bladder.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember the food's journey with: "Miss Emma Stole Sam's Lunch" — Mouth → Esophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine.
🍽️ Alimentary Canal vs. Accessory Organs
Alimentary CanalMouth, esophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine — about 20 feet long
Accessory OrgansSalivary glands, liver, pancreas, gall bladder — produce digestive juices
LiverLargest internal organ; produces bile for digesting fats
PancreasProduces insulin and other digestive enzymes
📚 Chapter Summary: The respiratory system brings oxygen into the body and removes carbon dioxide, through a path from the nose all the way down to the tiny air sacs called alveoli.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Follow the air's path with: "Naughty Puppies Love To Bark Loudly" — Nose → Pharynx → Trachea → Bronchi → bronchioLes → aLveoli.
🫁 Key Respiratory Structures
EpiglottisCloses when you swallow to keep food out of the lungs
LarynxThe voice box — produces sound
AlveoliTiny air sacs where oxygen and carbon dioxide are actually exchanged
📚 Chapter Summary: The circulatory system — heart, blood, and blood vessels — transports gases and nutrients throughout the body.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember blood vessel direction with: "Arteries Away, Veins to Ventricles" — Arteries carry blood Away from the heart, Veins carry it back.
🩸 Blood Components
Red Blood CellsCarry oxygen throughout the body
White Blood CellsFight infection
PlateletsForm clots to stop bleeding
PlasmaYellowish liquid, 90% water, carries food and collects waste
REMEMBER Heart chambers by animal: fish = 2, amphibians = 3, mammals & birds (and humans!) = 4. Jellyfish have no heart at all!
📚 Chapter Summary: The excretory system removes waste — carbon dioxide (lungs), solid waste (large intestine), sweat (skin), and liquid waste or urine (liver and kidneys).
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember urine's path out of the body with: "Kidneys Urge Bladder's Exit" — Kidneys (filter) → Ureters → Bladder (store) → urEthra (exit).
🚰 Key Excretory Facts
UreaA poisonous nitrogen compound made by the liver, removed in urine
NephronsTiny filtering structures inside the kidneys
📚 Chapter Summary: The nervous system is the body's control center, using fast electric signals. The endocrine system works more slowly, using chemical signals called hormones.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Nervous = Now (fast electric signals). Endocrine = Eventually (slower chemical signals/hormones). Both start with the speed they work at!
🧠 Nervous & Endocrine Highlights
CerebrumLargest part of the brain; right half controls the left side of the body, and vice versa
Pituitary GlandControls growth — height and body size
Adrenal GlandsProduce adrenaline for quick responses to fear or excitement
PancreasProduces insulin to regulate blood sugar
📚 Chapter Summary: The reproductive system allows living things to produce offspring, either through sexual reproduction (two parents) or asexual reproduction (one parent).
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Sperm + Egg = Zygote. Think: "S + E = Z" — the last letters of the alphabet, for the start of new life!
👶 Key Reproductive Terms
FertilizationWhen a sperm cell joins with an egg cell (ovum)
TestosteroneMale hormone, produced by the testes
Estrogen & ProgesteroneFemale hormones, produced by the ovaries
UterusMuscular organ that houses and nourishes a developing baby
✅ True or False — Key Reminders
FALSEOne organ alone can fully perform a major body function → organs need to work together as a system.
FALSEFixed joints allow a lot of movement → fixed joints (like in the cranium) allow no movement at all.
FALSECardiac muscles are found throughout the arms and legs → cardiac muscle is found only in the heart.
FALSEMelanin production decreases with UV exposure → it actually increases to help protect against sunburn.
FALSEThe epiglottis stays open when you swallow → it closes to keep food out of the lungs.
FALSEFish hearts have four chambers → fish hearts have only two chambers.

Part IV — Short Answer (sample answers)
51. Air enters the body through the nose or mouth and travels through the trachea, bronchi, and bronchioles until it reaches the alveoli in the lungs. Oxygen then passes into the blood through the capillaries and is carried by the blood to the cells of the body.
52. The sense organs collect information from the environment and send messages through nerve cells and nerves to the spinal cord and brain. The brain receives and interprets these messages, allowing the body to respond to changes in the environment.
53. Bones provide support and structure for the body, while muscles contract and relax to move the bones. Together, bones and muscles allow the body to move and perform different activities.
54. The respiratory system brings oxygen into the lungs and removes carbon dioxide. In the alveoli, oxygen passes into the blood, while carbon dioxide passes from the blood into the lungs. The circulatory system then transports oxygen-rich blood to the different parts of the body.
55. The digestive system breaks down food into nutrients. These nutrients are absorbed into the blood, and the circulatory system transports them to the different parts of the body where they are needed.
