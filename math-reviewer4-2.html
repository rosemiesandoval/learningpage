📖 Triangles & Quadrilaterals — Notes

📚 Chapter Summary: A polygon is named by its number of sides. A triangle has the fewest sides of any polygon — just 3.
🔷 Polygons and Their Sides
Triangle3 sides
Quadrilateral4 sides
Pentagon5 sides
Hexagon6 sides
Heptagon7 sides
Octagon8 sides
Nonagon9 sides
Decagon10 sides
📚 Chapter Summary: Triangles can be classified by their sides as equilateral, isosceles, or scalene.
TypeSidesExample
EquilateralAll three sides equal5 cm, 5 cm, 5 cm
IsoscelesTwo sides equal7 cm, 7 cm, 5 cm (base)
ScaleneNo sides equal6 cm, 9 cm, 10 cm
REMEMBER "Equi" means equal — an equilateral triangle has ALL sides equal, while an isosceles triangle has only TWO sides equal.
📚 Chapter Summary: Triangles can also be classified by their angles as acute, right, obtuse, or equiangular. The three angles of any triangle always add up to 180°.
TypeAnglesExample
AcuteAll three angles less than 90°60°, 70°, 50°
RightHas one 90° angle90°, plus two acute angles
ObtuseHas one angle greater than 90°109°, 50°, 30°
EquiangularAll three angles equal (60° each)60°, 60°, 60°
IMPORTANT All equilateral triangles are also equiangular triangles. And a triangle's classification by sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) is independent from its classification by angles (acute, right, obtuse) — they can combine in different ways!
📚 Chapter Summary: A quadrilateral is a four-sided polygon. It can be a general quadrilateral, trapezoid, parallelogram, or kite — and parallelograms split further into rectangles and rhombuses.
🔳 Classification of Quadrilaterals
General QuadrilateralNo pairs of parallel sides
TrapezoidOnly one pair of parallel sides
ParallelogramTwo pairs of opposite sides, both parallel and equal in measure
KiteTwo pairs of consecutive sides with the same measure
RectangleA parallelogram with four right angles
RhombusA parallelogram with four sides of the same measure
SquareA special rectangle with four sides of the same measure
REMEMBER A square is always a rectangle AND a rhombus — but a rectangle or rhombus isn't always a square! A trapezoid is NOT a parallelogram since it only has one pair of parallel sides.
✅ True or False — Key Reminders
FALSEAn obtuse triangle can be a right triangle → a triangle's angles add up to 180°, so it cannot have both an obtuse angle AND a right angle at once.
FALSEA quadrilateral is always a parallelogram → only quadrilaterals with two pairs of parallel sides are parallelograms.
FALSEA rhombus is always a square → a rhombus only needs four equal sides, not four right angles.
FALSEA trapezoid is a parallelogram → a trapezoid has only one pair of parallel sides, not two.
FALSEAn equiangular triangle has three equal angles, each measuring 90° → each angle actually measures 60°.
