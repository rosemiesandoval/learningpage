Point of View

A story’s point of view is the writer’s way of choosing who is telling the story to the readers. Stories are told through the narrator, who can be another character in the story or an observer that seems to see everything that happens.

These are the three points of view in a narration:

  • First person – In this point of view, the narrator is one of the characters telling events according to its way of looking. The pronouns I, me, we, and us are used by the narrator.

Call me Ishmael. Some years ago—never mind how long precisely—having little or no money in my purse, and nothing particular to interest me on shore, I thought I would sail about a little and see the watery part of the world.

—from Moby-Dick by Herman Melville

  • Second person – In this point of view, the narrator puts the spotlight to the reader, who becomes a character in the story. This point of view is rarely used. The pronoun you is mostly used by the narrator.

At the subway station you wait fifteen minutes on the platform for a train. Finally a local, enervated by graffiti, shuffles into the station. You get a seat and hoist a copy of the New York Post. The Post is the most shameful of your several addictions.

—from Bright Lights, Big City by Jay McInerney

  • Third person – This is the most common point of view used by writers. The narrator tells events as an observer and seems to know many things about the characters including thoughts and emotions. The pronouns he, she, it, they, him, her, and them are used by the narrator.

She gave him a look of the most intense admiration, and he thought it was because he had run away, but it was really because he knew fairies. Wendy had lived such a home life that to know fairies struck her as quite delightful.

—from Peter Pan by J. M. Barrie

Purpose of Writing

Writers have different purposes in writing. The purpose is the reason why the writer writes a text. There are many purposes for writing a text, but generally they are grouped into the following:

Purpose Description Example of Text
To inform or explain • used in presenting facts and information and in describing something
• related words: describe, define, report		 news article, product manual, textbook, encyclopedia
To entertain • used in amusing the readers by presenting a text creatively
• related words: narrate, relax, amuse		 short story, poem, novel, song, screenplay, playscript
To persuade • used in convincing the readers to take a stand or believe in a certain way
• related words: argue, defend, support		 opinion article, advertisement, campaign speech, movie review
To express • used in conveying the feelings and thoughts of the writer diary, social media post, personal journal, blog

Build New Knowledge

  • The narrator’s point of view allows the story to be told to the readers in a certain outlook.

  • Writers have different purposes for writing. The most common are to inform or explain, to entertain, to persuade, and to express.

Independent and Dependent Clauses

A complex sentence has two ideas with one serving as the main idea and the other dependent to it. The main idea is called the independent clause, and the other one is called a dependent clause.

An independent clause can stand on its own even without the dependent clause. However, the dependent clause does not have a complete meaning. Study these examples of complex sentences:

  • As they worked on their kites, they talked of the fights they would have.

  • Pedro was darting his kite up and down while Juan and Manuel were fighting their kites.

The underlined parts are dependent clauses, while the circled words (shown in bold above) are what you call subordinators. The rest of the sentence are independent clauses.

A dependent clause always has a subordinator attached to it. A subordinator allows the whole clause to be connected to the independent clause.

Common subordinators you can use are the following:

although, as, because, even if/though, if, since, so that, that, though, unless, until, when, whenever, where, wherever, while

Build New Knowledge

  • Complex sentences are made up of an independent clause and a dependent clause.

  • A subordinator allows a dependent clause to be connected to an independent clause.
