📚 Chapter Summary: Pronouns replace nouns so we do not repeat the same nouns. Personal pronouns refer to persons, animals, or things. Their case tells whether they are used as a subject (doing the action) or an object (the action is done to them).
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Subject = Starts the action. Object = Obtains the action. To check for an object, find the action verb and ask what it acts upon: "The father asked them to bring him a bundle of sticks."

Parts of a Letter

In the story you have read, Sonia’s grandmother wrote to her to ask her about how she was doing. Since they lived far away from each other, they had to communicate. Before texting through cell phones and sending chats and emails through the internet, most people communicated through writing letters.

A letter is a handwritten message by a sender to a receiver. Letters have many purposes. You can write letters to make a request, say thanks or an apology, inquire about something, file a complaint, or simply connect with each other.

Below are the basic parts of a letter:

  • Heading – tells the date when the sender wrote the letter; may also include the place where the letter was written

  • Greeting – mentions the name of the receiver; starts with “Dear” and ends with a comma

  • Body – contains the message

  • Closing – signals the end of the letter; ends with a comma

  • Signature – shows the handwritten name of the sender

Case of Personal Pronouns

You have already learned that pronouns are words used to replace a noun. If pronouns are not used, you will end up using the same nouns over and over in a sentence. Read this paragraph with no pronouns:

One day when the quarreling had been much more violent than usual and each of the sons was moping in a surly manner, the father asked one of the sons to bring the father a bundle of sticks. Then handing the bundle to each of his sons in turn the father told the sons to try to break the bundle. But although each one tried his best, none was able to do so.

What did you notice? Aren’t the nouns so many to read? Now read the paragraph again, this time with pronouns.

One day when the quarreling had been much more violent than usual and each of the sons was moping in a surly manner, the father asked one of them to bring him a bundle of sticks. Then handing the bundle to each of his sons in turn he told them to try to break it. But although each one tried his best, none was able to do so.

The underlined words are personal pronouns, which refer to persons, animals, or things. The case of a pronoun deals with how it is used in a sentence. It can be as a subject (the pronoun is doing the action) or as an object (the action is done to the pronoun).

Study these examples:

  • The father asked one of them to bring him a bundle of sticks.

  • They broke the sticks very easily.

  • If you agree with each other, enemies cannot injure you.

To tell if a pronoun acts as an object, look for the action verb that acts upon it.

Study these sentences:

  • Some boys stayed after class to clean the classroom.

Build New Knowledge

  • Personal pronouns replace specific persons, animals, or things.

  • Pronoun case tells if a pronoun is used as a subject or an object.

  • Indefinite pronouns do not identify or specify what they are referring to.

ANSWER KEY

Task 1

Circle the personal pronouns and box the indefinite pronouns.

  1. We had our trip last summer in Davao and [everyone] enjoyed it.

    • Personal pronouns: We, it

    • Indefinite pronoun: everyone

  2. Because of what happened, [many] are unwilling to follow her.

    • Personal pronoun: her

    • Indefinite pronoun: many

  3. These blankets helped us sleep soundly during the bus trip because they were comfortable.

    • Personal pronouns: us, they

  4. In Davao, my grandmother prepared durian and it was really delicious.

    • Personal pronoun: it

  5. Ms. Constantino asked [someone] to go to the office.

    • Indefinite pronoun: someone

  6. Only [a few] got low scores.

    • Indefinite pronoun: a few

  7. [Anything] can happen when you believe.

    • Personal pronoun: you

    • Indefinite pronoun: anything

  8. [Nobody] found the missing dog.

    • Indefinite pronoun: Nobody

  9. Omar wondered if [something] was wrong with the internet connection.

    • Indefinite pronoun: something

  10. Martina asked me to tell [everybody] that she would be ready soon.

  • Personal pronouns: me, she

  • Indefinite pronoun: everybody
