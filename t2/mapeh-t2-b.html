📚 Chapter Summary: Movement skills are basic motor skills. Locomotor skills move you from one place to another: jumping, hopping, running, walking, galloping. Manipulative skills use implements: throwing, catching, kicking, striking, dribbling.

📚 Chapter Summary: A melody is a series of musical sounds that form a complete, memorable idea: the "tune" of a song. It is shaped by pitch, rhythm, contour, phrasing, intervals, and scale and key. Melodic direction is how each note moves (up, down, or the same). Melodic shape is the overall path of the notes.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Melody ingredients = "P-R-C-P-I-S": Pitch, Rhythm, Contour, Phrasing, Intervals, Scale & key. Direction = "Up, Down, Same". Think of a hike: a hill = notes go up, a valley = notes go down, a flat path = notes repeat.
🎼 Six Ingredients of a Melody
PitchHow high or low the notes are
RhythmThe timing of the notes; gives the melody its pulse
Contour (shape)The overall up-and-down movement; adds emotional feel
PhrasingGrouping notes into smaller musical "sentences" called phrases
IntervalsThe distances between individual notes; affect the melody's character
Scale and keyThe set of notes and the central "home"; make the melody sound coherent

Games are activities that involve players moving around, with or without an object or implement, playing by a set of rules that have been mutually agreed upon. Games can be used for competitive play, cooperative play, or skill practice and self-testing. The four basic classifications are invasion or territory games, net or wall games, striking or fielding games, and target games. In this lesson, we will focus on a target game.

Target Games

Target games require players to hit something or someone (target) using an object to score. To hit this target, the players must throw the object precisely and with great control.

Target games require players to use a variety of manipulative skills, including throwing, catching, running, jumping, stretching, and dodging. One of the most popular target games in the Philippines is “batuhang-bola.”

Batuhang-Bola

Dodgeball originated from a deadly game among certain tribes in Africa. Tribe members used to play a game of throwing rocks in order to injure opponents. To avoid being injured, the tribe members must dodge the rocks. This served as training among tribe members so they could work together against other tribes. This game was brought by an English missionary to England, replacing rocks with rubber balls and creating simple rules. Eventually, the game reached the United States where new rules were added and the game gained wide popularity.

In the Philippines, the closest equivalent to dodgeball is called batuhang-bola. It is simpler than dodgeball in the United States. Two teams play in a rectangular court. The first team is divided into two and surrounds the court on both sides while the second team stands in the middle of the court.

Members of the first team on both sides take turns throwing the ball to hit members of the second team, who may run around within the court or dodge the ball. Any second-team member to be hit by the ball is out of the game. However, one remains in the game if he or she catches the ball with his or her hands; additionally, another team member who was eliminated gets to rejoin the game and play again. The goal of the game is for the first team to eliminate all members of the second team usually within a time limit.

Movement Concepts

In playing target games, you should be equipped with the proper concepts and skills. Knowledge and comprehension of movements are known as movement concepts, and they enable people to change and adjust their motion to meet specific movement goals.

Concepts related to movement include body awareness, space awareness, quality or effort, and movement relationships. When you become good at these things, you will be more confident in your body and be more motivated to try out and enjoy a variety of sports and activities. Thus, movement concepts can help you build a strong relationship with yourself, with others, and with objects.

Movement Relationships

The body’s connections with other things or with itself are revealed by movement relationships. The forms of connections that can happen when moving should be the focus of the relationship’s movement idea. In this idea, the connections between and among the movers or objects are more important than the “who” or “what” of a movement.

Begin learning the simplest concept to help you understand self-relationships (body parts, body shapes). The parts of our body can do many things. Your hands can do brushing and combing, and can touch objects around you. When you move using one body part to hold another body part and touch things around you, it is considered a relationship of body parts.

These are the examples of movement relationships of body parts and shape: round (curved), narrow, wide, twisted, and symmetrical/nonsymmetrical. These body parts and shapes can be used in different fun activities, such as word spelling using body parts, forming geometrical and animal figures, and dodging the ball in the game batuhang-bola. To avoid the ball, you have to move your body in any space while forming different shapes.

Below are movement relationships with whom or what the body is connecting while it moves.

  1. Round (Curved) – Make a round shape by curving the spine. Curl either forward or backward to create a circular shape. Forming round or curved shapes can help you avoid the ball in the game especially if it strikes in higher areas. Doing this makes sure that you have enough space and distance from the opponent, too.

  2. Narrow – To be as narrow as possible, stretch the body with your arms and legs assisting in forming the tight shape. It is used to occupy small spaces. You can use this shape when there are too many people in the play area. Form narrow shapes to create as much space between you and the other people or the ball.

  3. Wide – Keep your arms and legs away from the body center to create a wide shape. In doing this, make sure you have enough space on your sides. Check your distance from other players. Forming a wide shape can help you catch the ball.

  4. Twisted – You can form all sorts of twisted shapes by contorting some body parts from their natural positions. Twisting your body during a game will help you dodge the ball.

  5. Symmetrical and Nonsymmetrical – A symmetrical shape is produced when the figures on the two halves of the body are equal or the same. An asymmetrical shape has two halves of the body looking uneven or different from each other.

Movement Skills

Movement skills are the basic motor skills that involve different body parts. They are the primary motions to the more complicated movements used in games and certain sports.

You can further group different movement skills under locomotor and manipulative skills.

Locomotor Skills

These are movements performed by moving from one place to another.

  1. Jumping – You do this by springing from one foot or two feet and landing on both feet. Bending the knees allows you to do this comfortably. To jump forcefully, spring with your feet, swing your arms backward then forcefully forward and upward.

  2. Hopping – This is like jumping, except you only use one foot and land with the same foot. To hop comfortably, bend the leg and push from the toes to take off. Swing the arms back then forward and upward. More importantly, land on the toes first then on the ball of the foot while the knee is bent. Meanwhile, the other leg is bent throughout and swings to serve as a balance. (When you hop alternately each foot, you are doing skipping.)

  3. Running – Running consists of a series of forward-moving steps or strides that are continuous, fast, and long. In order to run correctly, you must maintain a slight forward lean while maintaining a straight back. Bend your arms, then let them swing freely at your sides while you move forward. The knees should be held high.

  4. Walking – To do this comfortably, lift and lower each foot alternately, moving at a regular pace. Step on the ground with your heel first then roll through your ball and toes. Finally, push out with your toes before lifting the foot for another step.

  5. Galloping – To do this, put one foot forward and the other one behind. Spring the front foot then lift the back foot forward, keeping the same foot in front and the other behind. As you do this, weight is transferred from the front foot to the back foot before the front foot lands on the next step. The feet move in one direction.

Manipulative Skills

These skills are done with the use of implements. These skills can also be a combination of locomotor and nonlocomotor movements that you can control.

  1. Throwing – This involves transferring an object to another side by forcefully hurling it through the air using an arm and a hand.

  2. Catching – This requires positioning a hand (or hands) at a certain spot where you expect a thrown object could be captured or received. You need to adjust the rest of the body, with good footwork, to be able to receive the object at the exact spot.

  3. Kicking – This is the act of striking an object (or someone) using the foot or shin (lower part of the leg).

  4. Striking – This involves forcefully hitting something using an object or a body part (usually the arm or the hand).

  5. Dribbling – This is the quick tapping or kicking of an object using the hands or feet, maintaining possession and control of the object.

Fitness Concepts

Physical fitness refers to the body’s capacity to cope with daily activities and adapt to the outside environment. A person who is physically fit is not only better able to perform daily tasks, but also has more energy to engage in leisure activities. The body should be able to adapt to environmental changes and pressures. Health-related physical fitness and sports-related physical fitness are the two categories of physical fitness. The following are the indicators of fitness:

  1. Coordination – This happens when the senses work in agreement with one or more body parts. This is the capacity of various body parts to work together simultaneously or at the same time. For example, you need a well-coordinated response of your body when playing batuhang-bola. Here you use your eyes, feet, and even arms in order to dodge the ball [?] or catch it.

  2. Balance – This is the ability to control the position of the body to remain steady either when moving or staying still. In playing batuhang-bola, for example, balance is needed to prevent the players from falling over when throwing the ball or dodging it. Leaping, hopping, and jumping while playing all require balance.

  3. Agility – This is the ability to quickly change position, direction, or speed in response to something. In playing batuhang-bola, having an agile body allows you to quickly move to dodge a fast-moving ball. You should be able to react too after seeing the movements of other players.

  4. Speed – This is the ability to quickly move the entire body or a portion of it. In playing batuhang-bola, speed is important in moving to retrieve a fallen ball and in throwing it again at an opponent. You can improve your speed or rate of movement through specific training.

Game Concepts

Technique or specific physical action skills are the focus of game concepts. Invasion games, court or net games, striking or fielding games, and target games are four categories of games based on how they are played. In playing these different games, you should understand the rules and movement principles based on the ideas of time and space:

  1. Maintaining possession of the ball – You can pass the ball or any other game object to a teammate using proper passing techniques (long pass, short pass, bounce pass, overhead pass, or high pass).

  2. Maintaining good communication with teammates – Ensure that your teammates can easily understand your signals before you pass the ball. This is essential for creating a strong play. Building a strong bond with your teammates is developed in practice sessions.

  3. Positioning – You need to know how to make and use space if you want to maintain ball possession. Make room for your teammates so they can move around as well. In a game, good use of space can help you win against your opponents.

  4. Regaining possession by throwing, dodging, or striking – Learn to throw or strike the ball properly to aim at your target. If you are in a playing area, apply the concept of relationship to other people and objects by performing the movement relationship of body parts to help you dodge or avoid the ball.

I Can Enhance My Skill

Play the game batuhang-bola as two sets of opposing players. Before you start, take note of the following safety precautions:

  • Excuse yourself if you are medically unfit to play a physical game.

  • Dress appropriately. Wear a loose shirt and jogging pants (or shorts).

  • Choose a throwing ball that is lightweight. A foam ball is recommended.

  • Do warm-up exercises before the game.

  • Players throwing the ball should aim for the bodies of opponents and not their heads.

  • Do not strike a player forcefully.

  • Maintain good sportsmanship when playing.

  • Do cool-down exercises after the game.

Mechanics:

  • Form two teams with four or six members each. Draw a rectangular court where the game will be played.

  • The throwing team will be called team A, while the target team will be team B.

  • Members of team A will be divided equally. They will line up on either side of the court.

  • Team B members will all line up in the center of the court.

  • During the game, members of team A will throw the ball at team B, whose players must try to dodge the ball in any way they can within the playing area. (Apply the movement skills you learned in this lesson.)

  • Team A members on either side of the court will take turns throwing the ball.

  • If a team B member is hit on any part of his or her body, that player is “out” of the game. He or she will stay in a designated space outside the court.

  • If a team B member manages to catch the ball, an “out” member rejoins the game once more.

  • Team A will win if none from team B remains in each game set. Otherwise, team B will win.

  • Each game set will last for five minutes. Each team will change roles after five minutes.

I Can Converse

Melody

A melody is a series of musical sounds that combine to form a complete and memorable idea. It is the “tune” of a song—the part you might hum, sing, or whistle. Think of a melody as a musical story. It is not just random notes; it is shaped by several key ingredients:

  • Pitch – How high or low the notes are. A melody moves between different pitches.

  • Rhythm – The timing of the notes—how long each sound lasts and how they are patterned. This gives the melody its pulse.

  • Contour (or Shape) – The overall up-and-down movement of the melody. Does it rise, fall, or wave? The melodic shape adds emotional feel to the music.

  • Phrasing – Melodies are often grouped into smaller, musical “sentences” called phrases, making them flow logically.

  • Intervals – The distances between individual notes, which affect the melody’s character.

  • Scale and Key – Most melodies follow a specific set of notes (a scale) and a central musical home (a key), which makes them sound coherent.

In essence, a melody is the main voice or story in a piece of music. It is what makes a song recognizable and can stir our emotions. While other parts like harmony (notes played together) and overall rhythm are important, the melody usually stands out as the most prominent and memorable element for most listeners.

Melodic Direction

This refers to the step-by-step movement of individual notes within a melody. Think of it as the immediate “path” from one note to the next.

Each note can do one of three things:

  • Go up: The note gets higher. (music staff example)

  • Go down: The note gets lower. (music staff example)

  • Stay the same: The note repeats itself, staying at the same pitch. (music staff example)

So, if you are looking at musical notes, you would observe if the line of notes is moving upward, downward, or staying flat from one to the next.

Melodic Shape

The melodic shape is like the path or the road the notes of a song take us on. Imagine you are going on a hike through a musical landscape. In this landscape, instead of walking on trails, you are following the path of a song. Sometimes, the path goes up a hill when the notes get higher. Other times, it goes down into a valley when the notes get lower. And sometimes, it is flat and straight when the notes stay the same. Just like every adventure has its path, every song has its unique melodic shape. This shape gives the song its special feel, whether it is happy and bouncy as you jump up the hills or sad and slow as you slide down into the valleys. Look at the samples of melodic shapes.

Melodies and Filipino Myths: A Journey through Sound and Story

“Sa Paglupad Ka Banog”: A Glimpse into Hinilawod

After learning how tunes move up and down through melodic directions and their shapes, you will now explore a story called “Sa Paglupad Ka Banog” (translated as The Flight of Banog), from a very long and old story called Hinilawod, an epic tale from the Visayas region, particularly among the Sulodnon people of Panay Island. Hinilawod is one of the longest epics recorded in the Philippines and is rich in themes of adventure, romance, and mythology. The word Hinilawod means “stories from the mouth of Halawod (also, Jalaud) River.” It is a big adventure about three superhero brothers, Labaw Donggon, Humadapnon, and Dumalapdap. They travel to magical places to have adventures and find their true love.

An adaptation of this story was made into a short film titled Sa Paglupad Ka Banog, cowritten and directed by Elvert Bañares. It won the Best Short Film at the 2023 Gawad Urian. It was shown at a big movie event in Iloilo City that celebrated movies from different places in the Philippines. The Darangen Epic: A Maranao Treasure

The Darangen, the epic poem of the Maranao people from Mindanao, is filled with captivating tales. Imagine a warrior parting a lake with a simple gesture, or chanting mysterious words to summon a giant ally in battle. This epic features powerful spells that can heal wounds, turn night into day, or even control animals. Every victory, every defeated beast, and every completed hunt in the Darangen showcases the heroes’ dedication to protecting their home by Lake Lanao.

At its heart, the Darangen tells a story of bravery, loyalty, and the search for justice. Through its musical tales, it explores profound themes of courage, love, and mysticism, beautifully showcasing the rich culture and traditions of the Maranao community.

Prince Bantugan is the central figure in the Darangen. He is known for his incredible strength, unmatched bravery, and skill in both fighting and diplomacy. His adventures are marked by heroic feats, battles with mythical creatures, and even journeys to the afterlife, all demonstrating his courage and wisdom.

Bantugan’s tales perfectly capture Maranao cultural values, highlighting honor, loyalty, and the importance of family and community. His character often represents ideal leadership, inspiring listeners with his ability to handle tough challenges, in both war and peace. Bantugan’s stories do not just entertain; they also teach important lessons and preserve the traditions and virtues of the Maranao people. Through his epic journey, the Darangen celebrates the strength of the human spirit and the lasting power of love and bravery, even when facing great difficulties.
