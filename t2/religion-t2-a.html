The Last Supper

The Lord Jesus, on the night he was betrayed, took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and said, “This is my body, which is for you; do this in remembrance of me.” In the same way, after supper he took the cup, saying, “This cup is the new covenant in my blood; do this, whenever you drink it, in remembrance of me.”

For whenever you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord’s death until he comes. (1 Corinthians 11:23–26)

2. What sacrament did Jesus establish at the Last Supper?

The sacrament of the Holy Eucharist is a community celebration of unity and love with Christ.

Unity means working together to achieve a common goal. It is setting aside our differences and self-centeredness; sharing our ideas, talents, gifts, and resources; and working together in harmony.

The Holy Eucharist is the third sacrament of initiation in which Jesus Christ gives His Body and Blood for us. He teaches us to give ourselves to Him in love and to be united with Him in Holy Communion. In this way, we are joined with the one Body of Christ, the Church. It is the source and summit of the Christian life. (LG 11; CCC 1322, 1324, 1325, 1409, 1413)

Jesus instituted the Holy Eucharist “on the night He was betrayed” when He gathered the apostles around Him and celebrated the Last Supper with them. (CCC 1323, 1337, 1340)

The Eucharist is sometimes referred to as Holy Sacrifice and Holy Mass. By offering ourselves with Christ in the Holy Mass, we unite ourselves with Christ.

The Eucharist is also referred to as the Lord’s Supper because every celebration of the Eucharist commemorates the supper that Christ celebrated with His disciples. At the same time, it is also the anticipation of the banquet that the Lord will celebrate at the end of time.

Similarly, the Eucharist is known as the Breaking of the Bread. It is the New Testament name for the Eucharist. It was an old Jewish ritual at meals, which Jesus employed at the Last Supper to express His gift of self for us (Romans 8:32).

The primary symbol in the Eucharist is the community or the people who are assembled or gathered together. They represent the Body of Christ.

The Mass consists of three rituals, namely Breaking the Bread, Sharing the Cup of Wine, and Proclaiming the Word of God.

1. Bread

Bread is the basic food for most people. It is both the gift of God and the work of human hands. It is made from wheat that has been ground, kneaded, and baked. In the Eucharist, the bread becomes the Body of Christ offered up, broken, and given to His people. As we partake of this Bread, we, too, are meant to be offered up, broken, and given for others.

2. Wine

Wine is also both the gift of God and the work of human hands. In the Eucharist, it is a symbol of the blood of Christ. When we partake of the wine, we celebrate the life-giving sacrament of Christ. We commit ourselves to pouring out our lives for others.

3. The Word of God

The Word of God is alive (Hebrews 4:12) and Jesus is present in the Scriptures. He is also present in the community of Christians gathered together. He comes alive to us whenever we hear the Word of God.

Mother Teresa (1910–1997)

Blessed Teresa of Calcutta once said:

“Your life must be woven around the Eucharist. Direct your eyes to Him, bring your hearts very close to His divine heart, ask Him for the grace to know Him, for the charity to love Him, for the courage to serve Him.”

With this reminder, ask for Christ’s grace and love. Recite the prayer together in class.

Early Life

Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu was born in Skopje, North Macedonia. Her family were Catholics and she was involved in the church from a young age. When she was 18, Agnes travelled to Ireland to become a nun and became known as Sister Teresa.

Becoming a Missionary

In 1929, the convent sent Sister Teresa to India as a missionary to promote the Catholic faith. She learned Hindi and Bengali and began teaching. In 1937, she took her final vows and became known as Mother Teresa.

Mother Teresa is remembered for devoting her life to helping the sick, poor and those in need.

“Not all of us can do great things, but we can all do small things with great love.”

A Message from God

In 1946, Mother Teresa believed God sent her a message telling her to leave teaching and go to Calcutta to help those most in need. She went to the poorest areas of the city to help the people who were overlooked by the rest of society. She and her group of fellow nuns had very little money or food for themselves and often had to beg.

In 1948, Mother Teresa set up a school for the poor and in the 1950s, she opened an orphanage for homeless children. In 1963, she founded the “Missionaries of Charity Brothers” to help very poor people who were not being cared for. During the 1950s and 1960s she also set up:

  • a leper colony

  • several mobile health clinics

  • a family clinic

  • a nursing home

Awards and Achievements

Mother Teresa was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979. In 1980, she was awarded the Bharat Ratna, India’s highest civilian award.

She was declared a saint in 2016 and is known as Saint Teresa of Calcutta.
