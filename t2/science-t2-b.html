Animal Habitats

An organism’s environment refers to all the things that surround it. In order to survive, an organism should be able to satisfy its needs, such as food, water, shelter, air, enough space, and protection from its enemies. Animals are found in places that have all the things that help them live. Such a place is called a habitat. A habitat is a place in which an organism lives.

Animal habitats may be grouped into terrestrial or land, aquatic or water, and aerial or above the ground.

Terrestrial Habitats

In a habitat, living things of the same kind live together in groups. There are times when several living things live with or near each other in a particular place. An example of this place is the forest. Many animals are found in the forest. This kind of habitat provides a lot of water. Forest animals live independently. They get food available in the forest, such as fruits, plants, insects, and other smaller animals. Animals get food, shelter, and protection in a forest.

Animals found in the forest:

  • Wild boar

  • Philippine eagle

  • Tamaraw

  • Philippine deer

  • Tarsier

Many animals live in a garden. Gardens are perfect places where animals, especially insects, can satisfy their needs for food. In a garden, you can find butterflies collecting nectar from one flower to another. You can also find grasshoppers, ants, birds, and worms. Under the ground, you will find earthworms and other burrowing insects like beetles, mealworms, scorpions, millipedes, and centipedes.

Animals found in a garden:

  • Butterfly

  • Ant

  • Worm

  • Bird

  • Centipedes

  • Beetles

  • Grasshopper

Domestic animals are usually found in a farm habitat. On a farm, farmers raise different animals, such as chickens, pigs, horses, carabaos, and cows. Ducks, goats, and sheep are also found on a farm. These animals usually provide food for people, such as milk, meat, and poultry products like eggs. Some animals are used for transportation like horses, cows, and carabaos. These bigger farm animals help farmers till the soil for planting.

Animals found on a farm:

  • Horse

  • Carabao

  • Cow

  • Pig

  • Sheep

  • Chicken

  • Duck

Deserts are very hot during the day. In order to avoid dehydration, desert animals stay away from the hot Sun. They dig holes or burrow in the ground for shelter. Kangaroo rats and lizards stay inside burrows and come out to look for food at night.

Desert animals have the ability to keep water in their bodies. Scorpions and wolf spiders have thick body coverings that help them prevent or reduce moisture loss. Their kidneys also concentrate urine so that they excrete less water.

Desert animals obtain water from succulent or watery plants like cacti. They also use the blood and body tissues of their prey as sources of water.

Examples of desert animals:

  • Centipede

  • Antelope

  • Cheetah

  • Eagle

  • Armadillo

Other examples of desert animals are the armadillos, ants, beetles, antelopes, centipedes, cheetahs, eagles, and snakes.

Aquatic Habitats

Aquatic habitats can be grouped into two: freshwater habitat and saltwater habitat. Many animals thrive in water. These animals possess unique parts and characteristics that enable them to adapt to their environment.

Freshwater Habitats

Freshwater animals are found in ponds, lakes, rivers, and streams. The kind of water environment determines the organisms living in these places.

Ponds are good for breeding freshwater animals like milkfish, tilapia, and shrimp. Rivers, lakes, and streams are home to mudfish, catfish, snails, frogs, and toads. Many other freshwater organisms live together in these places.

Some animals that live in freshwater:

  • catfish

  • frog

  • tilapia

  • snail

  • toad

Swamps are also considered freshwater habitats. Candaba Swamp, located in Pampanga, is a popular bird sanctuary for herons, wild ducks, Philippine ducks, kingfishers, egrets, and turtle doves. This habitat, which covers about 32,000 hectares of swamps, marshes, and ponds, is also home to thousands of migratory birds who are looking for warmer places to stay.

Saltwater Habitats

A large number of animals may be found in saltwater environments, such as seas and oceans. Due to the sheer size of oceans and seas, they are sanctuaries of plant and animal diversity.

Coral reefs are diverse habitats or ecosystems found under the sea. They contain about 25% of all marine organisms. Coral reefs serve as breeding places for seawater organisms.

Here, one can find sharks, sea turtles, different kinds of saltwater fishes, and eels. Tubbataha Reef in Puerto Princesa, Palawan, has been declared a natural park. Apo Reef, located in Occidental Mindoro, covers about 43 square kilometers of a reef system. It is one of the most beautiful diving destinations in the Philippines.

Another popular marine sanctuary is Bajo de Masinloc, located near Zambales. The place is known internationally as Scarborough Shoal. In the Philippines, Bajo de Masinloc or Panatag Shoal, forms a triangle-shaped chain of reefs and rocks. It covers an area of about 150 square kilometers. The place is rich with marine organisms, which is why Philippine fishermen frequent the place for fishing.

When freshwater rivers or streams mix with saltwater from the ocean, an estuary is formed. Estuaries are brackish, or slightly salty, areas that serve as home to many kinds of marine organisms, such as fishes, shellfishes, and some migratory birds.

Some areas near the shoreline are barely submerged, which is why part of it may be totally covered during high tide but partially uncovered during low tide. These places are called intertidal zones. These habitats are home to sea urchins, sea anemones, barnacles, crabs, different kinds of fishes, starfish, and mussels.

Mangrove forests are often found in intertidal zones. They also serve as breeding places of marine organisms. Most of the mangrove forests in the world are found in Asia. In the Philippines, you can find mangrove forests in Puerto Princesa and El Nido in Palawan, Mindoro, Siargao, Calauit Island, and Sibuyan Island. One can commonly find different kinds of fishes, jellyfish, mollusks, worms, barnacles, snails, crabs, and shrimps in these areas.

Bohol is rich with marine habitats, especially mangroves. The Banacon Island Mangrove Forest in Bohol is now an ecotourist destination. The area covers about 425 hectares of mangrove forest established through bayanihan among community residents. It is considered as one of the biggest mangrove plantations in the country.

Aerial Habitats

Some animals live in areas located above the ground. These aboveground dwellers climb and cling to parts of trees. They are also capable of transferring from one tree to another without necessarily getting on the ground. Trees and caves are considered aerial habitats. Some burrowing insects, birds with powerful beaks, and other organisms live in the trunks of trees.

BREAKING THROUGH LINK

Do you like animals? If you do, you might want to become a wildlife biologist. Wildlife biologists study wild animals and find ways of helping them survive. These biologists study and learn animal habits, what they eat, and where they go when they move from one place to another. They also study how long an animal could live so that they can suggest ways on how to sustain their population and save them from extinction. Wildlife biologists contribute a lot in the conservation of animal life.

Plant Habitats

Like animals, plants have needs in order to grow and survive in their environment. A need is something a living thing must have in order to continue living. Plants need air, water, food, and space. Plants also need light to make food.

Plants live in different places. Most plants grow on land. These plants obtain nutrients from the soil through their roots. Plants that grow on land are called terrestrial plants.

Terrestrial Habitats

Home gardens serve as home to many kinds of plants. The kinds of plants that can be found in a garden will depend on the interest of the gardener.

Some gardens have different kinds of ornamental plants. These plants have attractive and colorful leaves, such as bromeliads, mayanas, and snake plants. Many flowering plants are also found in gardens, such as roses, orchids, santan, lilies, and hydrangeas.

Some Ornamental Plants

  • Bromeliads

  • Santan plant

  • Snake plant

  • Red rose

  • Orchid

Since these plants are cultured, they need supplements in the form of fertilizers and good soil for them to grow healthy. Some plants need maximum sunlight. Other plants need only a small amount of sunlight. This is the reason why some plants grow best in shaded areas.

Some Fruits and Vegetables

  • Squash

  • Tomatoes

  • Raddish

  • Cabbage

  • Eggplant

  • Onions

Some gardeners prefer to have vegetable gardens. Vegetables are good sources of food. Some plants that can be found in a vegetable garden are squash, tomatoes, peppers, string beans, radish, cabbage, bitter gourd, peas, and eggplants. Herbs and spices such as onions, garlic, bay leaf, and basil can also be grown in a vegetable garden. Medicinal plants such as sambong, lagundi, ginger, and oregano are used to cure certain diseases and body illness. Plants like vegetables, when not sprayed with too much chemical insecticides, are healthy for the body. Some gardeners use organic fertilizers and pesticides to avoid the harmful effects of chemicals.

Rice fields and farmlands are planted with rice during the rainy season or when there is enough water supply to support their needs. During the dry season, rice fields are planted with different crops such as peanuts, corn, watermelon, mongo plants, turnips, cassava, and pineapple. Rice fields are also planted with vegetables for mass harvest and production.

Coastal areas contain sandy soil. These places are ideal for plants that need less water, such as coconuts, and trees that supply us with timber, lumber, and other hardwood for house construction and fuel.

Plants can also grow where the soil is wet like riverbanks, mangrove forests, swamps, and along the edges of ponds. Plants that grow in these places have thick taproots to hold the soil firmly, thus preventing soil erosion.

Grasslands are usually covered with all sorts of grasses. These plants can endure extreme heat. Grasslands are ideal for cattle and horse raising.

Some plants such as a cactus, can also grow in the desert. These plants have thick stems that allow them to store water for long periods of time.

Plants are also grown in controlled environments. These plants need constant monitoring and care. Examples of these habitats are plant nurseries and greenhouses. Hydroponic plants, such as lettuce, can be grown and cultivated without soil, but with water. Their habitats are enclosed to control the temperature, amount of heat, and space. Seedlings, cuttings, and newly transplanted and potted plants are kept in these places. A terrarium is another example of a controlled plant habitat.

Many wild plants and trees are found in the forest. These ecosystems are composed mainly of trees and plants that receive a high amount of rainfall. Most of the nutrients are obtained by trees, vines, and other wild plants, resulting in less fertile soil. This is why rainforests that are cleared for farming are good only for short-term crops.

In a rainforest, tall trees form a dense covering called canopy. The tallest trees that go beyond the canopy and make up the top layer of the forest form the emergent layer. The smaller plants and trees below the canopy make up the understory. Examples of plants that grow in a rainforest are narra, mahogany, birds of paradise, acacia, different varieties of bamboos, and palm trees.

Aquatic Habitats

Like animals, some plants can also live in water.

Plants that live in water are called aquatic plants or hydrophytes. Aquatic habitats can be grouped into flowing water habitats and standing water habitats. In flowing water habitats such as rivers and streams, water is in constant motion. In standing water habitats, such as ponds and wetlands, water does not move.

Ponds and Lakes

Ponds and lakes are bodies of standing water. A lake is bigger than a pond. Plants that live in these areas, such as lotus and azolla, have broad leaves and feathery roots that help them absorb water.

Wetlands

A wetland is a body of land that is usually flooded with water. Water in this place flows slowly into other bodies of water. Marshes and swamps are examples of wetlands. Water plants, such as water lilies and water hyacinths, grow in wetlands.

Rivers and Streams

Rainwater or water from a spring runs downhill and is collected in streams, creeks, or brooks. These watercourses merge into rivers whose water eventually ends up in a sea or an ocean. A smaller river flowing into a larger one is called a tributary and the area of land into which a river and its tributaries flow is called a watershed. Water lilies and water hyacinths grow in these bodies of water.

Oceans and Seas

For saltwater habitats, the seabed and ocean floors are sometimes covered with seagrass.

Aerial Habitats

Some plants live on the branches and stems of trees. They are called aerial plants or epiphytes. Aerial plants have specialized roots attached to other plants. Examples are orchids, bird’s nest plants, different kinds of ferns, and bromeliads. These plants have roots that are capable of absorbing water and moisture from the air.

TAKE NOTE

  1. Plants that grow on land are called terrestrial plants.

  2. Terrestrial plant habitats include the rainforests, deserts, gardens, coastal areas, and grasslands.

  3. Plants that grow in water are called aquatic plants or hydrophytes.

  4. Aquatic plant habitats include ponds and lakes, wetlands, rivers, and streams.

  5. Marshes and swamps are examples of wetlands.

  6. Some plants can grow on branches or stems of trees. These are called aerial plants or epiphytes.

WRAP-UP OF MAIN IDEAS

  1. A natural habitat provides enough food, water, shelter, and space for an organism.

  2. Animal and plant habitats are grouped into terrestrial, aquatic, and aerial.

  3. Freshwater habitats include ponds, rivers, lakes, swamps, and streams.

  4. Saltwater habitats include the sea, ocean, estuaries, mangrove forests, intertidal zones, and coral reefs.

  5. Terrestrial habitats include gardens, farms, coastal areas, grasslands, forests, and deserts.

  6. Aerial habitat refers to places where animals live above the ground.
