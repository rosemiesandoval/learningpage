📖 Read Aloud: Adam and Eve and the Garden of Eden

Christians believe that God created the Universe and everything in it. The Bible explains that God created humans in His own image. This means that in the beginning, everything was perfect and free from sin.

The first book of the Bible, Genesis, tells the story of the first people created by God, Adam and Eve. Many Christians believe that they lived together in a beautiful garden, called the Garden of Eden. They were created in the image of God. This means that they were without sin.

The Garden of Eden was very beautiful, with an abundance of fruit trees. God said to Adam and Eve that they could eat from any tree in the garden, apart from a tree called the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil. God said that if they were to eat fruit from this tree they would die.

One day, Adam and Eve noticed a snake in the garden. This serpent was actually Satan in disguise. He asked Adam and Eve, "Is it true that God has said you cannot eat the fruit from the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil?" "Yes," they answered. "God told us that we would die if we ate fruit from that tree." "You will not die!" said the serpent. "You will be like God, and you will know everything about good and evil."

Adam and Eve went to look at the fruit on the tree. It looked so ripe and delicious that, forgetting God's warning, they picked a piece of fruit and ate it.

God was very displeased with Adam and Eve for not following His instructions. They were given free choice by God and they disobeyed Him. Adam and Eve felt full of guilt and ran away from the beautiful Garden of Eden.

This first sin is sometimes known by Christians as 'Original Sin.' The relationship between humans and God was changed and Christians believe that, from this point onwards, no human was free from sin.

— Genesis (from the Forgiveness slides)

📖 Read Aloud: Jesus Forgives and Heals a Paralyzed Man

One day Jesus was teaching, and Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there. They had come from every village of Galilee and from Judea and Jerusalem. And the power of the Lord was with Jesus to heal the sick. Some men came carrying a paralyzed man on a mat and tried to take him into the house to lay him before Jesus. When they could not find a way to do this because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and lowered him on his mat through the tiles into the middle of the crowd, right in front of Jesus.

When Jesus saw their faith, he said, "Friend, your sins are forgiven."

The Pharisees and the teachers of the law began thinking to themselves, "Who is this fellow who speaks blasphemy? Who can forgive sins but God alone?"

Jesus knew what they were thinking and asked, "Why are you thinking these things in your hearts? Which is easier to say, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up and walk'? Sin: Kinds and the 7 Deadly Sins

Sin is bad and it turns us away from God
2 kinds of sins
1. Venial sin – less serious or light sin
2. Mortal sin – grave or serious sin

7 deadly sin
1. Lust – unrestrained sexual craving
2. Gluttony – to over indulge, especially by over – eating
3. Pride – inflated sense of one’s accomplishments
4. Sloth – laziness or lack of effort
5. Wrath – uncontented feelings or hatred and anger
6. Greed – the excessive desire of material things
7. Envy – Jealousy towards another’s happiness.

Jesus Forgives and Heals a Paralyzed Man

One day Jesus was teaching, and Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there. They had come from every village of Galilee and from Judea and Jerusalem. And the power of the Lord was with Jesus to heal the sick. Some men came carrying a paralyzed man on a mat and tried to take him into the house to lay him before Jesus. When they could not find a way to do this because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and lowered him on his mat through the tiles into the middle of the crowd, right in front of Jesus.

When Jesus saw their faith, he said, “Friend, your sins are forgiven.”

The Pharisees and the teachers of the law began thinking to themselves, “Who is this fellow who speaks blasphemy? Who can forgive sins but God alone?”

Jesus knew what they were thinking and asked, “Why are you thinking these things in your hearts? Which is easier to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up and walk’? But I want you to know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins.” So he said to the paralyzed man, “I tell you, get up, take your mat and go home.” Immediately he stood up in front of them, took what he had been lying on and went home praising God. Everyone was amazed and gave praise to God. They were filled with awe and said, “We have seen remarkable things today.” (Luke 5:17–26)

Explanation

In the Gospel story, Jesus is presented as one who has the power to heal. He has power over sickness and sin. He healed people physically by curing their illnesses and healed spiritually by forgiving their sins.

Many people who witnessed Jesus’ compassion, healing, and forgiveness recognized the presence and ways of God in His actions. Jesus is indeed the sign of God’s healing love. God was at work in and through Jesus. “Christ’s compassion toward the sick and his many healings of every kind of infirmity are a resplendent sign that God has visited his people and that the kingdom of God is close at hand. Jesus has the power not only to heal, but also to forgive sins; he has come to heal the whole man, soul and body; he is the physician the sick have need of” (CCC 1503).

The Church believes that Jesus continues to bring God’s healing power. Jesus continues to touch and heal us through the sacraments. As Christians, we are called to reach out to those in need and “recognize the need of the sick for special help and comfort from family, community, and God’s grace” (CCC 1870).

Sin and Forgiveness

Sin is a free decision to do what is bad and turn away from God. The Catholic Church teaches that there are two kinds of sins: venial sins and mortal sins.

A venial sin may be described as a less serious or light sin. It is done without full knowledge or complete consent. It weakens love for God and others.

A mortal sin is a grave or serious sin. It is committed with full knowledge and deliberate consent. It is a grave violation of God’s law. It turns a person away from God. (CCC 1854–1863)

God forgives sins, be they big or small. Our sins are forgiven through confession. We make God happy when we confess our sins sincerely. We confess them with sorrow and faith in God. We resolve not to commit sin again.

God waits for us to come to Him and be sorry for our sins. He is willing to forgive and love us although we often hurt Him through our sins. His grace abounds and His mercy endures forever.

Jesus teaches us to give mercy rather than to condemn sinners and to show understanding rather than judgment. He calls for conversion or change of heart. He invites us to return to God. He strengthens us to turn away from sin.

Reconciliation is a sacrament of healing. Through it, Jesus embraces and heals us. It brings us freedom, peace, and joy. We receive forgiveness from God for sins that we commit after baptism. We are reconciled with God and the Church. (CCC 1468–1470)

As members of the Church, everything we do affects the whole Church. Sin wounds and hurts the Church. The healing we receive in reconciliation restores health and strength to the Church and to ourselves.

The Essential Elements in the Sacrament of Reconciliation

The following are the essential elements in the sacrament of reconciliation:

1. Examination of conscience is a methodical and prayerful review of one’s life and actions to determine how he or she conforms to God’s will.

2. Contrition is a heartfelt sorrow for the sins committed with the intention of sinning no more.

3. Confession is telling sins to the priest.

4. Penance is the atonement that the priest imposes on the sinner. It can be a prayer or action done to repair the hurt committed against God and others.

5. Absolution is a prayer spoken by the priest for the forgiveness of sins.

B. Contrition

Contrition is an important element in the sacrament of reconciliation. Contrition means that we are sorry for our sins. It makes us see the contradiction between God’s love and sin. It leads us to change for the better and to place all our hope in God. Recite this prayer to express sorrow for your sins.

Act of Contrition

My God, I am sorry for my sins with all my heart.

In choosing to do wrong and failing to do good,

I have sinned against You Whom I should love above all things.

I firmly intend with Your help to do penance,

to sin no more, and to avoid whatever leads me to sin.

Our Savior Jesus Christ suffered and died for us.

In His name, my God, have mercy.

(Revised Rite of Penance)

Key Question: How did you decide which things were right and which things were wrong? You have probably grown up with the people around you teaching you the difference. Perhaps this was your parents, your teachers or family friends. Christians believe that God teaches them what is right and what is wrong, both in the stories of the Old Testament and through the teachings of Jesus. A sin is a word Christians use for something that is wrong and against the word of God.

Is God Forgiving: Key Vocabulary

  • forgive / forgiveness: Christians believe that Jesus showed them that God is always ready to forgive. This means that if Christians say sorry for sins they will be forgiven by God.
  • permanent: When something is permanent, it means it is forever and it can not be changed.
  • sin: In Christianity, a sin refers to anything that goes against God. This can be wrong actions, thoughts or attitudes.

What Is a Sin? Key Question: What do Christians believe about sin? Christians believe that God created the Universe and everything in it. The Bible explains that God created humans in His own image. This means that in the beginning, everything was perfect and free from sin.

Adam and Eve The first book of the Bible, Genesis, tells the story of the first people created by God, Adam and Eve. Many Christians believe that they lived together in a beautiful garden, called the Garden of Eden. They were created in the image of God. This means that they were without sin.

The Garden of Eden The Garden of Eden was very beautiful, with an abundance of fruit trees. God said to Adam and Eve that they could eat from any tree in the garden, apart from a tree called the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil. God said that if they were to eat fruit from this tree they would die.

Original Sin One day, Adam and Eve noticed a snake in the garden. This serpent was actually Satan in disguise. He asked Adam and Eve, "Is it true that God has said you cannot eat the fruit from the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil?" "Yes," they answered. "God told us that we would die if we ate fruit from that tree." "You will not die!" said the serpent. "You will be like God, and you will know everything about good and evil."

Adam and Eve went to look at the fruit on the tree. It looked so ripe and delicious that, forgetting God's warning, they picked a piece of fruit and ate it.

God was very displeased with Adam and Eve for not following His instructions. They were given free choice by God and they disobeyed Him. Adam and Eve felt full of guilt and ran away from the beautiful Garden of Eden. This first sin is sometimes known by Christians as 'Original Sin.' The relationship between humans and God was changed and Christians believe that, from this point onwards, no human was free from sin.

Christians believe that God sent His son, Jesus, to the world to take the punishment for all of mankind's sins. Jesus died on a cross and rose again to teach Christians that they can be forgiven if they put their trust in Him.

What Is Forgiveness? When Jesus was killed on the cross, He said, 'Forgive them Father, for they do not know what they do.' (Luke 23:24) Key Question: What do you think about what Jesus said? Would you say the same?

Forgiveness is permanent: Christians believe that God's forgiveness lasts forever. This means that once He has forgiven a sin, the slate is wiped clean and that sin is forgotten in His eyes.

Forgiveness is free: Christians believe that they don't have to do anything to earn or pay for forgiveness. It is always available and given freely by God.

Forgiving Others: Christians also believe that they should try to forgive others, just as God forgives them. This is often difficult but it is something many Christians strive to do in their lives, using Jesus as an example. 'Forgive them Father, for they do not know what they do.'

Is there anything you think that God would not forgive? Why? Talk about this with your partner.

Forgiveness Let's think about the discussions we have had about forgiveness. Key Question: Which sins did you feel were more difficult to forgive and why?

Christians believe that God will forgive any sin, no matter how big or small. To do this, Christians put their trust in God and in the message of Jesus. For Christians, God's forgiveness is free and it is permanent. This is one of the main teachings of Christianity.
