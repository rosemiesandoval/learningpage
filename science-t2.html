The Nervous System

When you play and work, the different parts of your body move. This is because the different organ systems work with the nervous system.

The nervous system consists of three major organs: the brain, spinal cord, and nerves.

The brain is divided into three major regions: cerebrum, cerebellum, and brain stem.

The brain is the control center of the body in humans and most animals. The spinal cord is the part of the nervous system connected to the brain. From the spinal cord are the nerve cells, which branch out to the different parts of the body. Each part of the brain has a specialized function. One part of the brain controls thinking and receives information from the different sense organs. Another part controls breathing, heartbeat, and digestion. The cerebrum, the largest part of the brain, is divided into two parts. The right half controls the left side of your body, while the left part controls your right side.

Nerve cells are present throughout the body. These cells are so sensitive that they can transmit messages rapidly to the brain. The brain interprets the message which causes you to react. Some nerve cells allow you to feel cold temperatures. Others allow you to tell that something is warm. Some nerve cells let you feel pain and pressure.

Together, the nerve cells and the different sense organs help you detect and understand changes in the environment.

The Endocrine System

Have you ever felt excited while watching a game? When this happens, your heart beats faster than usual. This is because of chemicals in the body called hormones. Hormones are chemical messengers that tell certain organs what to do. They are produced by the endocrine system. The endocrine system is composed of glands that produce hormones that regulate bodily functions.

The pituitary gland controls the way you grow. This gland is located just below your brain. The hormones from this gland carry messages that tell your cells to grow. This gland is responsible for increases in height and body size.

The adrenal glands are a pair of glands that sit on top of each kidney. These glands produce the hormone adrenaline, the hormone that helps you respond quickly when you are scared, excited, or facing a threat.

The pancreas produces insulin, a hormone which regulates the amount of sugar in the body.

The testes produce testosterone, the hormone that facilitates the development of male secondary characteristics, while the ovaries produce estrogen and progesterone, the hormones that facilitate the development of female secondary sex characteristics.

For Reproduction

Reproduction is the process by which an organism produces offspring of its own kind. Reproduction is important so that an organism will continue living its kind from one generation to generation.

The Reproductive System

There are two main ways living things can make new ones: sexual reproduction and asexual reproduction.

Sexual reproduction involves two parents. The male parent contributes a special cell called a sperm, while the female parent contributes an egg cell called an ovum. When a sperm meets and joins with an ovum in a process called fertilization, it starts the growth of a new organism.

Asexual reproduction only needs one parent. This parent can make a copy of itself in different ways, without needing a sperm or egg.

The Male Reproductive System

The testes produce a hormone known as testosterone. This hormone is responsible for the changes that take place in the male body. The scrotum protects the testes. This protective sac is important to maintain a cooler temperature compared to the rest of the body.

Sperm cells are produced in the testes. Nutrient-rich fluids protect the sperm and at the same time provide a medium for transport. The mixture of fluids and sperm is called semen. Sperm matures in the epididymis before it travels through the vas deferens and then to the urethra of the penis, from where it is expelled.

The Female Reproductive System

Changes in the female body are caused by a hormone called estrogen. It is produced inside the ovaries.

The ovaries are small, oval-shaped organs attached on either side of the uterus. Each month, one of the ovaries produces a mature egg cell. When the mature egg cell unites with a sperm cell, fertilization takes place. The fertilized egg, called a zygote, moves to the uterus.

The uterus is the muscular organ that houses and nourishes the egg until it develops into a baby.

Unfertilized egg cell is released through the vagina or birth canal, in a process called menstruation.

Biochemists

The average life span of humans has significantly increased over the last few centuries due to advancements in technology, healthcare, sanitation, nutrition, and public health measures. While new diseases have emerged, advancements in the field of medical science have also led to improved ways of treating diseases.

Biochemists study the biochemical process in biological systems, such as human and animal organ systems. Their studies contribute to the overall improvement of medical treatments and techniques.

Key Points / Summary

  1. The nervous system is the control center of the body.
  2. The endocrine system controls the chemical signals that trigger different bodily functions.
  3. The reproductive system is responsible for the propagation of life.

Plants are living things that can make their own food with the help of sunlight. They grow well in places where there is enough light, water, and nutrients. Plants have different structures or body parts that help them survive. Plant parts are divided into two groups: the root system and the shoot system. Each system has parts performing specific functions to help the plant survive.

Root System

The root system anchors a plant in place. Most grow underground and absorb water and nutrients. The root system holds plants firmly to the ground. Some keep plants in place, like orchids, which grow on trees. It can also act as a storage of excess food.

Roots vary. Some roots are big, hard, and long. Some are short, soft, and tiny. Roots that grow close to the surface, and are soft and tiny are called fibrous roots. Grasses have fibrous roots.

Some roots are thick, fleshy, and strong. They grow deep in the soil with smaller rootlets that branch out. These roots are called taproots. Sometimes taproots are used to store food. When you eat a carrot, radish, gabi, or beet, you are eating a taproot.

Many rain forest plants such as epiphytes, grow high on trees. They cling to the stems and branches of other bigger plants. These plants have roots above the ground capable of absorbing moisture and nutrients from the air. Some aboveground roots start on the plant stem and grow down into the soil. Examples of these plants are orchids, bromeliads, and some ferns.

Shoot System

The shoot system of a plant consists of the aboveground parts of the plant. This includes the stem, branches, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds. The shoot system is important in the food-making process and in propagation.

Stem

The stem is a structure that supports the plant. It has specialized tissues called xylem and phloem. The xylem allows the transport of water from the roots to the upper parts of the plant, while the phloem facilitates transport of food throughout the plant.

Stems can be woody or herbaceous. Woody stems are hard and strong. Most trees have woody stems and a main stem called trunk. The trunk can withstand strong winds and rains. Rings underneath the bark indicate a tree’s age. Herbaceous stems are soft. Beans and tomatoes have herbaceous stems.

Some plants have ways to protect themselves in their respective environments. Bougainvillea plants have thorns in their stems to prevent animals from eating them. A thorn is a modified branch or stem of a plant. Some plants have stems with spines, which are protective modified leaves, and prickles. Most desert plants have thick succulent stems. Since water is scarce in these areas, these plants have stems capable of storing large amounts of water. The plants use the stored water in order to survive in a hot environment.

Leaves are the parts of plants that make food through the process of photosynthesis. During this process, the green substance in the leaf called chlorophyll reacts with carbon dioxide, with the help of sunlight and water, to produce sugar. Another product of this process is the oxygen we breathe. Humans and animals survive because of the oxygen that plants provide.

A leaf is attached to the stem by the petiole. The petiole connects to the midrib or primary vein, which is the central vein of the leaf that provides structure and sends water and food to the rest of the leaf through the secondary veins. The expanded, flat portion of the leaf is the leaf blade or lamina. The edge of the leaf is known as the margin, while its tip is known as the apex.

Leaf shapes also vary among plants. Some plants such as pine trees and other coniferous plants have needlelike leaves. Other plants have broad, heart-shaped, oval leaves. The outer layer of a leaf is a waxy layer called cuticle. This layer helps plants in regulating loss of water.

Flowers, Fruits, and Seeds

Flowers are the most attractive parts of plants. They are so colorful that they attract insects and some birds to gather nectar, a sugar-rich liquid. Insects pollinate flowers. Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower. The male sex cell from the pollen grain will travel along the style to reach the ovule in the ovary. These cells will unite to produce seeds. Seeds are then enclosed in a ripened ovary called fruit. Some fruits are edible.

Fruits contain seeds that help plants propagate. Seeds are scattered in many ways. They can be scattered through wind, water, animals, and people, or through special structures such as hooks and spines. Plants like the amor seco have spines that cling to the skin of animals and to people’s clothes. Plants such as mahogany, ipil-ipil, and madre de cacao have fruits that explode when dried. The explosive mechanism causes the seeds to scatter. When seeds fall on fertile soil, they sprout and grow into new plants.

Structures That Help Aquatic Plants Survive

Aquatic plants grow in water environments such as ponds, paddies, rivers, lakes, and marshes. These plants are called hydrophytes. Many aquatic plants are either totally or partially submerged in water. Other hydrophytes such as the lotus and the water lily float on water. These plants have flat leaves that serve as floating structures. In some cases, aquatic plants have hairy or feathery root systems that enable them to absorb water continuously. The leaves require much water so the roots should be capable of absorbing water.

Key Points / Summary

  1. Plants possess certain structures or body parts that help them survive.
  2. Roots support and anchor plants into the soil. They absorb water and minerals from the soil, and store food in the form of starch.
  3. Fibrous roots branch out in all directions.
  4. A taproot is a thick main root that grows deep into the ground.
  5. Epiphytes have aboveground roots capable of absorbing nutrients and moisture from the air.
  6. Stems have vascular tissues called xylem and phloem which transport water and food, respectively.
  7. Plants have structures, such as thorns, spikes, and prickles, that protect them from animals.
  8. Photosynthesis is the food-making process in plants. During this process, sugar is produced while oxygen is given off.
  9. Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower.

C. Explain or discuss the following.

  1. What would happen if trees have a fibrous root system? Why?

Answer: The tree could easily fall over. Fibrous roots are short, soft, and tiny, and they grow close to the surface. A tree is big and heavy, so it needs deep, strong roots like a taproot to hold it firmly in the ground, especially during strong winds and rains. Shallow roots also can’t reach water deep in the soil.

  1. What characteristic helps flowering plants ensure pollination?

Answer: Flowers are colorful and they have nectar, a sweet liquid. These attract insects and some birds. When the insects and birds visit to get the nectar, they carry pollen from one flower to another, which helps pollination happen.

  1. How are spines, hooks, and the explosive mechanisms in seeds helpful in plant propagation?

Answer: They help scatter the seeds to different places. Spines and hooks cling to the skin of animals and to people’s clothes, so the seeds are carried far away (like the amor seco). Fruits with explosive mechanisms burst open when dried and throw the seeds out (like mahogany, ipil-ipil, and madre de cacao). When the seeds land on fertile soil, they grow into new plants.

Key Points / Summary

The nervous system is the control center of the body. The endocrine system controls the chemical signals that trigger different bodily functions. The reproductive system is responsible for the propagation of life. A plant is made up of a root system and a shoot system. The root system of a plant can be taproot or fibrous. The shoot system contains the stem, branch, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds. Different processes take place within the root system and shoot system in plants.

D. Answer the following questions.

  1. What happens if an organ in the human body fails to work?

Answer: The body will not work properly and the person can get sick. The organs and organ systems work together, so if one organ fails, the other organs are affected too. If it is a very important organ like the heart, brain, or lungs, it can be dangerous to the person’s life.

  1. Why do doctors always recommend to have regular exercise and a balanced diet to their patients?

Answer: Regular exercise and a balanced diet keep the body’s organs healthy and strong. Exercise makes the heart, lungs, muscles, and bones stronger. A balanced diet gives the body the nutrients it needs for energy, growth, and fighting sickness. Together they help prevent diseases.

Part VI — Short Answer (sample answers)
47. The tree could easily fall over. Fibrous roots are short, soft, and tiny, and they grow close to the surface. A tree needs deep, strong roots like a taproot to hold it firmly in the ground, especially during strong winds and rains.
48. Flowers are colorful and have nectar, a sweet liquid, which attracts insects and birds. When they visit to get the nectar, they carry pollen from one flower to another, helping pollination happen.
49. They help scatter seeds to different places. Spines and hooks cling to animal skin and clothing, carrying seeds far away. Fruits with explosive mechanisms burst open when dried, throwing seeds out. When seeds land on fertile soil, they grow into new plants.
50. The body will not work properly and the person can get sick. Organs and organ systems work together, so if one organ fails, others are affected too. If it's a vital organ like the heart, brain, or lungs, it can be dangerous to the person's life.
51. Regular exercise and a balanced diet keep the body's organs healthy and strong. Exercise makes the heart, lungs, muscles, and bones stronger. A balanced diet gives the body nutrients for energy, growth, and fighting sickness.
52. We can eat these plant parts, and they give us energy and nutrients. Examples: Roots (carrot, radish, gabi, beet), Stems (sugarcane, kangkong, potato), Leaves (pechay, malunggay, cabbage), Fruits (mango, banana, papaya).
