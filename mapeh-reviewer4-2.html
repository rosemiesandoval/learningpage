📖 Art & Hygiene — Notes

📚 Chapter Summary: The elements of art are the building blocks artists mix and match to create artwork — line, shape, color, texture, space, form, and value.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember the 7 elements (Line, Shape, Color, Texture, Space, Form, Value) with this silly sentence:
"Little Sammy Colors The Sky, Framing Views."
Line · Shape · Color · Texture · Space · Form · Value
🖌️ The 7 Elements of Art
LineA moving point; can be straight, curved, wavy, or jagged
ShapeFormed when lines connect; two-dimensional (length + width)
ColorAn aspect of light — hue, brightness (value), and intensity
TextureHow something feels (or seems to feel) when touched
SpaceWhere objects are placed; creates a sense of depth
FormShapes that look three-dimensional (length + width + height)
ValueThe lightness or darkness of a color
📚 Chapter Summary: The principles of art describe how an artist organizes the elements. This lesson covers three: balance, proportion, and movement.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Remember the 3 principles (Balance, Proportion, Movement) with:
"Big Paintings Move!" — Balance, Proportion, Movement
PrincipleMeaning
BalanceVisual stability — symmetry (same both sides), asymmetry (contrasting sides), or radial symmetry (around a point)
ProportionThe size relationship between parts of an artwork, compared using a scale
MovementHow the artwork guides your eyes to travel across it, suggesting action
📚 Chapter Summary: Filipino folk art reflects the elements and principles of art through local traditions: the bul-ul, burnay, táka, and sarimanok.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Match each folk art to its home region using this pattern: "Bul-ul Blesses, Burnay Bakes, Táka Trims, Sarimanok Soars."
Bul-ul → Ifugao rice guardians (wood)  ·  Burnay → Vigan jars (clay)  ·  Táka → Paete figures (paper)  ·  Sarimanok → Maranao bird (wood/metal/paint)
🏺 The Four Folk Art Forms
Bul-ulWooden rice guardian figures of the Ifugao people, made from narra or molave, ritually blessed
Burnay (Bornay)Unglazed earthen jars from Vigan, Ilocos Sur, baked in a dragon kiln for 3 days
TákaPapier-mâché figures from Paete, Laguna, made with gawgaw paste over wooden molds
SarimanokMythical bird of the Maranao people, decorated with okir carving designs, holding a fish
📚 Chapter Summary: Hygiene is a set of practices to keep yourself clean. Washing your hands properly, caring for your fingernails, and keeping your face clean all help prevent the spread of disease.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK Scrub your hands for 20 seconds — that's about how long it takes to sing "Happy Birthday" twice! 🎂🎂
🧼 5 Steps to Wash Your Hands
1. WetWet your hands with clean water
2. LatherUse soap and rub your hands together
3. ScrubPalms, backs, between fingers, under nails — for 20 seconds
4. RinseRinse hands completely with water
5. DryPat your hands dry
REMEMBER Keep fingernails short and clean underneath — never bite them! And always wash your hands before touching your face.
📚 Chapter Summary: Good hygiene covers your whole body — how you cough or sneeze, how you care for your feet, hair and scalp, and genital area.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK When you cough or sneeze, aim for your elbow, not your hands — think "vampire cough"! 🧛 Covering with your elbow (instead of your hands) keeps germs off the things you touch next.
🦶 Body Hygiene Reminders
Coughing/SneezingCover with your inner elbow or a clean tissue; wash hands after
Foot HygieneWash and dry feet well — bacteria and fungi thrive in moist areas
Hair & ScalpBathe daily, shampoo regularly; don't share combs or towels
Genital HygieneWash with gentle soap; wear clean cotton underwear; don't hold in pee
📚 Chapter Summary: Round out your hygiene routine with your teeth, ears, and clothes — each has its own simple daily habits.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK The "2-2-2 Rule" for oral hygiene: Brush 2 times a day, for 2 minutes, and see the dentist 2 times a year!
🦷 Oral, Ear & Clothes Hygiene
BrushingShort strokes, circular motions, don't forget the tongue
ToothbrushReplace every 3-4 months
Ear CareNever insert anything into the ear canal — earwax protects your ear naturally
ClothesChange daily, wash with detergent, rinse thoroughly
REMEMBER If it can't go in your mouth, it shouldn't go in your ear either — not even a cotton bud!
✅ True or False — Key Reminders

FALSEBacteria and fungi thrive best in dry areas of the feet → they thrive in moist areas.
FALSEBornay jars are glazed → they are unglazed, with no glossy coating.
FALSETáka figures are made from carved wood → they're made from paper pulp (papier-mâché).
FALSEIt is fine to bite your fingernails → this spreads germs and should be avoided.
FALSEToothbrushes should be replaced only once a year → every 3 to 4 months instead.
